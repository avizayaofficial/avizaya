School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 1

Your healing begins when you stop abandoning yourself.

Your healing begins not when they come back. Not when someone finally sees you. Not when life gets easier. It begins the moment you decide not to leave yourself again.

"Though my father and mother forsake me, the Lord will receive me."

Psalm 27:10 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

I need you to know something before a single word of this reaches you.

I am not teaching this from a distance. I am not reading research about abandonment from a comfortable chair. I have lived inside this wound. I know what it feels like to be 16 years old and so desperate to disappear that you make plans for it. I know what it feels like to stand in front of a mirror and hate what you see so completely that you punish your own body for existing. I know what it feels like to give everything you have and still go to bed feeling like something essential is missing, wondering what is wrong with you.

Nothing was wrong with me. And nothing is wrong with you.

But something happened. And that something has a name. And once you can name it, you can begin to heal it.

The Bible has known this name for thousands of years. Long before psychology gave it language, God was already speaking to the abandoned woman. He was already reaching into the places where people had left her and saying something that no human being had ever said to her before.

I see you. I have not forgotten you. Your name is written on my hands.

That is where we begin.

Part 1

What abandonment actually is

Most people think abandonment means someone left. A parent walked out. A man disappeared. A friend went silent. And yes, those things are abandonment. But the wound I want to talk about today goes deeper than the people who left you. I want to talk about the moment you left yourself. Because that is the abandonment that keeps the wound open. Not the one they did to you. The one you learned to do to yourself in response.

"The first person who abandoned you taught you how to abandon yourself. And you have been practicing ever since."

God tells us in Psalm 27:10, "Though my father and mother forsake me, the Lord will receive me." Notice what He does not say. He does not say it will not hurt. He does not say you will not feel the weight of that leaving. He says: even when the people who were supposed to stay do not, I will receive you. The abandonment of others does not have the final word over your life. But you have to come to Him first. And most of us never come to Him because we are too busy going back to the people who left, trying to get from them what only He can give.

"Though my father and mother forsake me, the Lord will receive me."

Psalm 27:10 NIV

Here is what self-abandonment looks like. Read this slowly, because most women do not recognize it in themselves. They only recognize it in other people.

Self-abandonment is saying yes when your whole body is screaming no. It is shrinking yourself so someone else feels comfortable. It is apologizing for existing in a room. It is going hungry so others can eat, going silent so others can speak, going without so others can have. It is explaining yourself endlessly to people who were never listening. It is staying in places that are costing you your peace because you are more afraid of being alone than you are of being destroyed slowly. It is the eating disorder. It is the nights you spent praying to disappear. It is the relationships where you poured until there was nothing left and still kept pouring.

That is what self-abandonment looks like. And it starts, almost always, with someone who left.

Part 2

How it gets inside you

When someone who was supposed to stay leaves, your nervous system does not process it as their failure. It processes it as evidence. Evidence about you. About your worth. About whether you are the kind of person who gets to be loved and chosen and stayed for.

Your brain, especially the brain of a child or a teenager, does not have the capacity to say, "That person had wounds that had nothing to do with me." It says something much simpler and much more dangerous. It says: "If I was enough, they would have stayed."

This is the lie that the enemy plants in the wound. And it is a very specific lie. Because look at what it does. It takes their failure and turns it into your identity. It takes what they did and converts it into who you are.

The Bible calls this out directly. John 8:44 says the devil "is a liar and the father of lies." The lie that you are not enough, that you are unworthy of being stayed for, that there is something fundamentally wrong with you that causes people to leave, that is not a truth you discovered. That is a lie you were handed in a moment of pain. And you have been carrying it as if it were yours ever since.

"He was a murderer from the beginning, not holding to the truth, for there is no truth in him. When he lies, he speaks his native language, for he is a liar and the father of lies."

John 8:44 NIV

And from that one lie, a whole architecture of self-abandonment gets built. You learn to make yourself smaller so people will not leave again. You learn to give more, forgive faster, need less. You learn to read rooms and moods and silences so you can adjust yourself before someone has the chance to walk away. You learn that your needs are a burden. That your emotions are too much. That love is something you earn by performance, not something you receive by simply existing.

And then you become an adult, carrying all of that, and you wonder why you feel so empty. Why the relationships do not last. Why you keep giving everything and receiving nothing.

The biology of abandonment This is not just spiritual or emotional. Science has confirmed what God already knew: abandonment and rejection activate the same neural pathways in the brain as physical pain. When someone left and it felt like it was going to kill you, that was not weakness. That was your body doing exactly what it was designed to do. Your body was not lying. Understanding this is where compassion for yourself begins.
Part 3

The woman at the well

I want to take you to one of the most powerful encounters in the entire Bible. John chapter 4. The woman at the well.

She comes to draw water in the middle of the day. Not in the morning with the other women. In the heat of noon, when no one else is there. Bible scholars tell us this was deliberate. She was avoiding people. She had been married five times and was now living with a man who was not her husband. In her culture, in her time, she would have been looked at as a woman who could not keep a man. A woman who had been left, over and over, until the leaving became her identity.

She comes to the well alone. Ashamed. Trying to get what she needs without being seen. And Jesus is already there, waiting for her.

"When a Samaritan woman came to draw water, Jesus said to her, 'Will you give me a drink?'... The Samaritan woman said to him, 'You are a Jew and I am a Samaritan woman. How can you ask me for a drink?'"

John 4:7,9 NIV

She is shocked that He speaks to her at all. Two categories of person that Jesus, a Jewish rabbi, would have had no cultural reason to address. A Samaritan. And a woman. She had learned to make herself invisible. She had learned that she did not deserve to be spoken to, let alone seen.

But Jesus does not look past her. He looks directly at her. He knows everything about her, every man who left, every wound she is carrying, every way she has tried to fill the emptiness with something that could not hold it. And He does not condemn her for any of it. He offers her living water.

"Everyone who drinks this water will be thirsty again, but whoever drinks the water I give them will never thirst. Indeed, the water I give them will become in them a spring of water welling up to eternal life."

John 4:13-14 NIV

Here is what Jesus is saying to her, and to you. You have been going back to the same wells. The same people, the same relationships, the same patterns, the same attempts to fill the void. And every time you drink from those wells you are thirsty again. Not because you are broken. Because those wells were never designed to hold what you are thirsty for.

The abandonment wound keeps reopening because you keep returning to human sources to heal what only God can heal.

The woman at the well left her water jar at the well. She did not need it anymore. She had found something that would never run dry. And then she went back into the town she had been hiding from, and she told everyone she met about the man who knew everything she had ever done and loved her anyway.

That is what this healing looks like. You stop carrying the jar. And you become the testimony.

Part 4

What you have been doing to yourself without realising it

I want to ask you something and I want you to sit with it before you answer. When was the last time you chose yourself?

Not performed self-care. Not bought something. Not posted something. Chose yourself. Stayed when it was hard. Kept the promise you made to yourself. Said no to something that was costing you your peace. Spoke up for your own needs in a room where no one was asking.

Proverbs 4:23 says, "Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it." Above all else. Not above your relationships. Not above your reputation. Not above making other people comfortable. Above all else, guard your heart.

That is not selfishness. That is stewardship. You cannot pour from an empty vessel. You cannot give what you do not have. You cannot build what you are too depleted to carry.

"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."

Proverbs 4:23 NIV

"You cannot pour someone into maturity. You can only pour until you are empty. And emptiness was never your calling."

This is the pattern. Someone leaves. You decide the solution is to give more. You give more. They still leave, or they stay and take everything you give without returning it. You pour more. Your body starts breaking down. Your energy disappears. You call it burnout or depression or exhaustion but underneath it all is the same girl who decided at some point that her worth was measured by how much she could give before she finally collapsed.

The Bible has a name for this too. In 1 Kings 19, the prophet Elijah has just done something miraculous for God. And then he sits down under a tree and asks to die. He is utterly depleted. He says, "I have had enough, Lord." And what does God do? He does not rebuke him. He does not tell him to get up and keep working. He sends an angel to feed him. Twice. And says, "Get up and eat, for the journey is too great for you."

"Then he lay down under the bush and fell asleep. All at once an angel touched him and said, 'Get up and eat. The journey is too great for you.'"

1 Kings 19:5,7 NIV

God knew that Elijah could not continue from empty. And He knows that you cannot either. Choosing yourself is not a betrayal of others. It is obedience to the God who created the vessel and knows exactly what it needs to keep carrying His purpose.

Part 5

The spiritual root of the wound

Here is what I believe with everything in me. The reason self-abandonment feels so natural, the reason we slide into it without even noticing, is because we have not yet built our identity on the one foundation that cannot be moved.

Most of us have built our identity on other people. On whether they stayed or left. On whether they approved or rejected. On whether they saw us or looked through us. And so every time a person leaves, a piece of our identity leaves with them. Because we had given them the power to define us.

But Romans 8:38-39 makes one of the most devastating declarations in all of Scripture. "I am convinced that neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

"Neither death nor life, neither angels nor demons, neither the present nor the future, nor any powers, neither height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God that is in Christ Jesus our Lord."

Romans 8:38-39 NIV

Paul lists every possible force that could threaten to separate you from love. Death. Life. Angels. Demons. Time. Power. Space. And then, as if that were not enough, he adds: anything else in all creation. The list is exhaustive because God knew we would try to find exceptions.

God knew we would say: but what about what they did. What about how badly I was left. What about how broken I am. And the answer is the same. Nothing. Not one thing. Not one person, not one wound, not one act of abandonment can separate you from this love.

"All these years I was waiting for God to change my life while God was waiting for me to become willing to change."

When you build your identity in who you are before God, before your roles, before your relationships, before what you have achieved or failed to achieve, you become unshakeable. Not because life stops being hard. But because you are no longer dependent on other people staying in order to know who you are.

Part 6

What healing actually looks like

I want to be honest with you about healing, because I think most of what we hear about healing is a lie dressed up in aesthetics. Candles and journaling and morning routines are not healing. They can support healing. But they are not it.

Real healing is ugly. It is sitting with the pain you have been numbing for years and feeling it for the first time without reaching for the thing that makes it stop. It is saying no for the first time and watching someone be disappointed with you and choosing to survive it. It is choosing yourself in a small way on a Tuesday when no one is watching.

The Bible calls this process sanctification. It is not instant. It is not comfortable. Romans 12:2 says, "Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind." The word transformed in Greek is metamorphoo. The same word we get metamorphosis from. A caterpillar does not transform without first going into a dark, enclosed, uncomfortable place. It does not emerge looking the same. And it cannot return to what it was.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is — his good, pleasing and perfect will."

Romans 12:2 NIV

Healing is when the little girl inside you, the one who decided she was not enough the day someone walked away, starts to believe something different. Not because someone finally told her she was worthy. But because you, the adult woman, started showing her with your choices.

Every time you keep a promise to yourself, your self-worth expands. Every time you stay at your own table, your identity gets stronger. Every time you choose your peace over someone else's approval, you are sending a message to every wounded part of you that says: I am here. I am not leaving. You are safe now.

That is the healing.

Five questions to sit with this week

You do not need to answer these all at once. Read one a day. Write if you can. Bring them to God in prayer if that is your language.

One. Where am I still abandoning myself? In which relationships, which conversations, which daily choices am I still choosing someone else's comfort over my own truth?

Two. What was the first abandonment I remember? Not necessarily a person leaving physically. When was the first time I learned that my needs were a burden?

Three. What have I been using to numb what I have not yet felt? Food, scrolling, overworking, overgiving, alcohol, relationships that take everything. What am I reaching for to avoid what is underneath?

Four. Like the woman at the well, what jar am I still carrying that I was never meant to carry? What would it look like to leave it behind?

Five. If the little girl I was could see how I am treating myself right now, what would she feel?

The kintsugi practice

Kintsugi is the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with gold. The philosophy is this: the break is not something to hide. It is something to honour. The repaired vessel is more beautiful because it carries the evidence of what it survived.

This is also the theology of the cross. God does not erase the scars. He redeems them. Jesus rose with the wounds still in His hands. Thomas touched them. The wounds became the proof of the resurrection, not the shame of the crucifixion.

This week, try this.

  1. Find a quiet moment alone. Sit down. Put your hand on your chest and feel your own heartbeat. That heartbeat is not an accident. God breathed life into you on purpose. Genesis 2:7.
  2. Say out loud, even if your voice shakes: "I am not broken. I am being repaired. And the repair is the most beautiful part of me."
  3. Name one place where you have been abandoning yourself. Say it out loud. No punishment, no shame. Just naming it. "I have been abandoning myself by..." Naming it is the first act of choosing yourself.
  4. Make one promise to yourself for this week. Not a list. One promise. Something small and keepable. And keep it. That kept promise is your first act of coming home to yourself.

A prayer for the woman beginning this journey

God, I am here.

I have been running for a long time. From the pain, from myself, from You. And I am tired of running.

I do not fully understand this healing yet. I do not know what it will cost or what it will require of me. But I am willing to begin.

Show me where I have been abandoning myself. Show me the places where I learned that I was not enough. And then show me the truth You have held about me since before I was born.

You told Isaiah that even if a mother forgot her child, You would not forget me. That my name is engraved on Your hands. Let me know that today. Not as information. As a reality I can feel.

Rebuild me. Not back to who I was before I was broken. Into something more beautiful than I could have been without the breaking.

Teach me that I am safe now. That the healing has already begun. That I am not too late.

Amen.

A note before you go

I want to say something to the woman who is reading this and feeling like it is too late. The woman who is 60 years old and has spent a lifetime abandoning herself. The woman who is 17 and still in the middle of it. The woman who is 35 and has just begun to understand why her relationships keep breaking apart. The orphan who never had anyone to tell her she was worth staying for.

It is not too late. Healing does not have an expiry date. Joel 2:25 says God will restore the years the locusts have eaten. Not some of the years. The years. Every season that was stolen from you, every year you spent in the wrong place with the wrong people doing the wrong version of yourself, God says He can restore it. That is not wishful thinking. That is a covenant promise.

"I will repay you for the years the locusts have eaten."

Joel 2:25 NIV

You survived the abandonment. Now it is time to stop re-enacting it on yourself.

You are not the girl who was left behind. You are the woman who decided to stay.

Your healing begins not when they come back.
Not when someone finally sees you.
Not when life gets easier.
It begins the moment you decide not to leave yourself again.
