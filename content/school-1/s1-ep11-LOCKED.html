School 1: The abandoned girl  ·  Episode 11

Forgiving the woman in the mirror.

A teaching on the forgiveness she has refused herself for years, and the freedom that arrives the moment she finally extends grace to the woman who is reading these words.

Welcome

Of all the forgiveness work a daughter of God will ever do, the forgiveness of herself is almost always the hardest, the longest, and the one she most often skips. She has learned to forgive her father. She has learned to forgive her mother. She has learned, sometimes, to forgive the man who failed her. But the woman in the mirror has been carrying a judgment from her own self that has gone untouched by mercy for years, and until she lays that judgment down, no other healing will fully settle in her.

She has built a court inside her own mind, and she has been the prosecutor, the jury and the judge all at once. The verdict has been guilty, the sentence has been long, and the punishment has been the constant low-grade self-rejection she has carried into every relationship and every season of her life. She does not even hear the verdict being spoken anymore, because it has been speaking under her thoughts for so long that she has confused it for her own voice. But it has not been her voice. It has been the voice of an old judgment she has been refusing to overturn, and today the overturning begins.

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Romans 8:1 NIV

Paul is naming a verdict that the daughter of God needs to receive with the same force she received the day she first believed. No condemnation. None. Not for what she did when she was younger. Not for what she allowed when she did not know better. Not for what she stayed in long after she should have left. Not for what she gave away that she now wishes she had kept. The Lord has placed her, by the work of the Cross, in a position where condemnation no longer has any legal hold on her, and yet she has been carrying a condemnation she manufactured herself, against herself, with no spiritual authority behind it whatsoever.

This episode is the work of finally aligning her inner verdict with the verdict the Lord has already spoken over her. The shift from self-condemnation to self-forgiveness is not a single moment, it is a practice, but it begins with a decision, and the decision is what this teaching invites her to make today.

Part 1

The judgments she has been carrying.

Before a woman can forgive herself, she has to name what she has been holding against herself, because the judgments she carries against herself are often unconscious, and the unconscious judgments are the ones that do the most damage.

She has been holding it against herself that she stayed too long. In the relationship, in the friendship, in the job, in the season she now knows in her bones was costing her her peace. She walks past the version of herself who stayed and she shakes her head at her, and the head-shaking has become its own kind of low-grade self-violence, repeated quietly every time she remembers what she allowed.

She has been holding it against herself that she gave too much. She rehearses the gifts, the time, the money, the energy, the love she poured into people who poured none of it back, and the rehearsing makes her feel like a fool. She does not yet have the language to see her giving as a virtue that was simply misplaced, so she has been interpreting the giving itself as the failure, when really the failure was only the placement.

She has been holding it against herself that she did not see the signs. She replays the moments when, in hindsight, the truth was obvious, and she punishes the version of herself who did not yet have the eyes to see what she now sees clearly. She forgets that vision is built over time, that wisdom is the fruit of experience, and that what is obvious to her now was not obvious to her then because she had not yet lived through what made it obvious.

She has been holding it against herself for the addictions, the avoidance, the years she spent numbing what she did not yet know how to feel. She has been calling that survival weak, when really it was the very thing that kept her alive long enough to reach the moment she could finally heal it.

She has been holding it against herself for the relationships she should have ended, the conversations she should have had, the truths she should have spoken. The list of should-haves has become so long that she has stopped trying to address it, and instead she carries the whole pile as a vague but persistent sense that she has been failing herself for years.

Read those five paragraphs slowly, dear woman, and let yourself see, perhaps for the first time, how much you have been carrying against yourself. The unconscious has to become conscious before it can be laid down, and the laying down is the work of this whole episode.

Part 2

The two voices in the mirror.

There is a moment in the life of every woman of God when she catches her own reflection and the moment becomes a kind of small confrontation between two voices that have been arguing inside her for years. One voice is the voice of accusation, and the other is the voice of the Father, and the voice she chooses to agree with becomes the voice that defines the next chapter of her life.

The voice of accusation says, “Look at what you have allowed. Look at who you became. Look at how much time you wasted. Look at the woman you should have been by now.” The accuser does not give her credit for any of the growth. The accuser does not acknowledge any of the strength it took to get to this very moment. The accuser only catalogs the failures, and the catalog is the verdict the accuser has been pressing on her for years.

The voice of the Father says something completely different. The voice of the Father says, “Look at how far you have come. Look at what you have survived. Look at how much I have been forming in you, even in the seasons you thought I had forgotten you. Look at who you are becoming. There is no condemnation here. There is only the love of a Father who has been working in you, and who is not finished.”

Both voices have been speaking to you in the mirror. The work is not to silence the voice of accusation through force of will, but to agree with the voice of the Father so consistently and so loudly that the accusing voice loses its authority by the simple fact that you are no longer reinforcing it.

The Word of God names the accuser by his title.

"For the accuser of our brothers and sisters, who accuses them before our God day and night, has been hurled down."

Revelation 12:10 NIV

The accuser is not a metaphor. The accuser is a spiritual reality, and the accuser has been speaking against the daughters of God since the beginning. The verse is the great news that the accuser has already been hurled down, which means the accusations he is still trying to whisper have no spiritual authority over the daughter who knows whose she is. She does not have to argue with him. She only has to recognize that the voice making the case against her is not the voice of her Father, and her Father’s voice is the only voice that has the authority to set or change her standing.

The inversion

You were not stupid. You were loyal.

Darling, there is a single reframe that, when a woman finally receives it, ends the long inner argument she has been having with the woman she used to be, and replaces the accusation with the truth.

You were not stupid, you were loyal, hopeful and loving, and you did not deserve what happened to you. The very qualities that made you stay are the very qualities that make you the woman the Lord has been forming all along.

The accuser has been telling you for years that what you allowed makes you a fool. The truth is that what you allowed reveals a heart that was capable of staying, hoping, believing, and forgiving in proportions most people are not capable of. Those are not weaknesses. Those are the very capacities that, once rightly placed, will become the most powerful gifts of your life. The problem was never your heart. The problem was where you placed your heart. The placement is what you are now learning to do differently. The heart itself was never the issue.

This reframe matters because the accusation of stupidity has been holding the door of self-forgiveness closed for years. As long as a woman believes she was a fool, she will keep punishing the version of herself who made the choices the foolishness implied. The moment she sees that she was not a fool but a loyal and hopeful woman who simply did not yet have the discernment she has now, the punishment loses its rationale, and the forgiveness becomes possible for the first time.

The principle

I was loyal, hopeful and loving. I did not deserve this. The woman I was did the best she could with what she knew, and she is worthy of the same grace I have extended to others.

Part 3

The forgiveness Jesus multiplied.

Peter once asked Jesus a question that has been asked, in some form, by every daughter of God who has tried to forgive someone who hurt her again and again. He asked how many times he was required to forgive the same offense.

"Then Peter came to Jesus and asked, ‘Lord, how many times shall I forgive my brother or sister who sins against me? Up to seven times?’ Jesus answered, ‘I tell you, not seven times, but seventy-seven times.’"

Matthew 18:21-22 NIV

Peter was offering what he thought was a generous answer. Seven was already a complete number in the Hebrew imagination, the number of fullness, of perfection. Surely seven was enough. Surely seven exhausted what could reasonably be asked of him. And Jesus multiplied the number to seventy-seven, which is not really a number at all, it is a way of saying without end. Forgive again. Forgive again. Forgive again. Without limit.

There is something in the very word that the daughter of God needs to hear. To forgive is to for-give. To give away. Forgiveness is the deliberate releasing, the giving away, of what has been held inside her. She is not absolving the act. She is not pretending the harm did not happen. She is for-giving the spirit behind the harm. She is releasing it from her own body, her own heart, her own mind, so that it no longer has a place to live inside her.

This is why unforgiveness is so costly. The spirit that caused the harm, when it is not released, finds a home in the very person it harmed, and it begins to operate from inside her, slowly shaping her into a version of the very thing she was wounded by. Bitterness, suspicion, hardness, vigilance, the closing of the heart. These are not character flaws. They are the residue of a spirit she never gave away, that has been living inside her ever since the wounding, quietly forming her into its image.

To for-give yourself is the same work. The judgment you have been carrying against yourself is a spirit, and so is the accusation, the shame, the self-condemnation. Each of them is alive inside you, and each of them has been quietly shaping you into a version of the woman they are accusing you of being. To for-give yourself is to give all of it away, to release it from your body, to refuse to host it any longer. The giving away is the actual work of the forgiveness, and the forgiveness is the giving away.

The teaching is most often applied to forgiving others, but the principle is just as true for forgiving yourself. The woman in the mirror does not need to be forgiven once. She needs to be forgiven again every time the old voice of accusation returns. Every time the memory surfaces. Every time the regret tries to climb back into the present. Forgive her again. Forgive her again. Forgive her again. Until the forgiveness becomes the dominant voice and the accusation becomes the voice that has lost its place.

True justice lies within forgiveness, because forgiveness is the only thing that breaks the cycle of the accuser’s influence over the woman’s life. As long as she keeps prosecuting herself, the accuser has somewhere to whisper. The moment she chooses, finally, to release the woman she used to be, the accuser loses his foothold, and she walks free into the rest of her life.

There is a piece of the parable in Matthew 18 that often gets missed, and it is the most sobering piece of the entire teaching. When the wicked servant refused to pass on the mercy he had been given, his master delivered him to the jailers until he should pay everything he owed.

"In anger his master handed him over to the jailers to be tortured, until he should pay back all he owed. This is how my heavenly Father will treat each of you unless you forgive your brother or sister from your heart."

Matthew 18:34-35 NIV

What the Lord is naming through this parable is a spiritual law that the daughter of God has to understand if she is going to walk in freedom. When a woman refuses to forgive, the spirit of unforgiveness is given permission to abide inside her, and it operates like the jailers in the parable, tormenting her until the forgiveness finally comes from her heart. The torment is not God being cruel. The torment is the natural consequence of hosting a spirit that was never designed to live inside her. As long as she holds the unforgiveness, the unforgiveness holds her.

And here is the question that the servant in the parable could not answer, and that every daughter of God has to face in her own way. If he is in jail, how can he pay back the debt? He cannot. He has no income in jail. The very state of his imprisonment makes the repayment impossible. And the same is true of the woman in the spiritual jail of unforgiveness. As long as that spirit lives inside her, she cannot earn her way out, because the imprisonment itself is what prevents her from doing the very thing that would free her.

The way out, in the parable, was never about money. The money was already forgiven. The way out was that the servant needed to pass the same grace forward, to give to his fellow servant what the master had given to him. The Lord is naming the same exchange for every daughter of God. She does not pay back her freedom through earning. She pays it forward through forgiveness. She gives away the grace that has been given to her, and the giving away is what releases her from the jail.

"Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you."

Ephesians 4:32 NIV

Paul is naming the principle in the cleanest possible form. We forgive because God first forgave us. The forgiveness flows out of what was already given to us, not out of any obligation we are trying to manufacture from within ourselves. Christ has already paid the price for every failure the daughter of God has ever committed, and the forgiveness she has been refusing to extend to herself is forgiveness the Father has already extended to her. The work is not to earn it. The work is to receive it and pass it forward.

This is also the place to name something important about what forgiveness actually is and what it is not. Forgiveness is not about forgiving the act itself, because the act in itself is often unforgivable. Forgiveness is about forgiving the person who did the act, because God has already forgiven the person. The act stands as wrong forever. The person who committed the act has been granted mercy by a Father who paid the full price for the wrong to be released. The same applies to the woman in the mirror. The acts she committed in the past may stand as wrong, but the woman who committed them has been forgiven, fully and completely, by the Cross. Your job is to forgive the woman, not to relitigate the acts.

And here is the question that ends every long inner argument about forgiveness, the question the daughter of God has to answer honestly before she can walk into the next chapter of her life. Do you want to be free? Because until you forgive, you will never be free, and if you want to be free, you have to forgive. Time does not heal without forgiveness. Time only makes the spirit of unforgiveness grow inside you, and the pain the person caused, including the pain you have caused yourself, grows even deeper with the passing years. The forgiveness is the doorway. Nothing else will open it.

You do not forgive for them. You forgive for you. The release of the spirit of unforgiveness is the release of yourself from a jail that was never meant to hold you, and the giving away of the grace you have been given is the very mechanism by which the jail door swings open and the rest of your life walks free.

Part 4

The hidden sweetness of the martyr.

There is a piece of this teaching that goes deeper than the standard work of self-forgiveness, and the daughter of God who is serious about her healing has to be willing to receive it, because the deepest layer of self-forgiveness involves admitting something that is harder than admitting the failure itself. It involves admitting that, somewhere quietly inside her, she liked it.

She liked being the one who gave everything and got nothing. She liked occupying the moral high ground that being the giver provided her. She liked being able to say, even silently to herself, “I did everything. They did nothing. I am the good one.”

The martyrdom is a clean identity. It makes her the innocent victim. It removes from her any responsibility for the situation. It allows her to feel injured and righteous at the same time, and that combination is more emotionally satisfying than the woman of God wants to admit. The martyr is an addictive identity for a wounded woman, and a particularly hard one to release, because releasing it requires her to come down from the high ground and admit that she was also playing a role in the dynamic.

This is not self-blame. This is self-compassion in its deepest form. You were not a victim of forces entirely outside your control. You were a woman participating in a pattern that you were also benefiting from, even when the benefit was only the cleanness of being the one who was wronged. Admitting this does not undo the harm that was done to you. It only restores your agency, and the restoration of agency is the doorway to a real healing that the martyr identity cannot enter.

Why did the martyr identity feel good? Because pain was familiar and being undervalued was home, and because when you were the giver you were in control, and you never had to be vulnerable enough to just receive. You could hide behind the help you were offering, and you could hide behind the narrative that you were sacrificing for love. True vulnerability is not giving. True vulnerability is receiving without a receipt, letting someone else pay, being okay with being seen in your need.

The deepest self-forgiveness comes when you forgive yourself for liking the pain. Not because the pain was good. The pain was real and the harm was real. But because admitting that part of you was also choosing the dynamic is the only way to step out of it for the rest of your life. Let the martyr die. She got you out alive. But she cannot enter the promised land of reciprocity, and the promised land is where the Lord is calling you next.

Part 5

Forgiveness is not free.

There is one more piece of this teaching that the daughter of God needs to receive, because there is a counterfeit understanding of forgiveness in the culture that has been making the real thing harder than it needs to be. The culture has been suggesting that forgiveness is something light, something that costs nothing, something a woman can extend with a quick decision and a brief prayer. That is not what the Word of God names as forgiveness, and the misunderstanding is part of why so many women have been struggling to forgive themselves and others for years.

Forgiveness is not free. Never call it free. Christ had to die on a Cross for the forgiveness of sins to be made available to the world, and the price was His very body. Every drop of blood that fell from Him was the cost of the freedom you now have access to, and the forgiveness you offer to yourself or to anyone else is rooted in that exact payment. The forgiveness flows freely now, but only because the price was already paid in full by Someone who was willing to bear what you could not bear yourself.

And the forgiveness you extend, in your own life, also has a cost. It costs you the satisfaction of being right. It costs you the moral high ground of remaining the wounded one. It costs you the energy you have been spending on the case against the person, or against yourself. It costs you the part of your heart that has been holding the verdict closed. None of that is light. Anyone who has truly forgiven knows that the forgiveness was one of the hardest things she ever did, because the cost was real, and the cost had to be paid, and the paying was the very work of the forgiveness itself.

Forgiveness is a gift that you give. The person receiving it gets it for free, but you, the one extending it, pay something to be able to extend it at all. This is also true of the forgiveness you extend to yourself. The woman in the mirror receives the gift, but you, the woman extending it, have to pay something to give it. What you pay is the cost of laying down the case you have been building against her. The case feels precious because you have spent years constructing it, but it is the very thing keeping you imprisoned, and the laying down of it is the price of your freedom.

Forgiveness must not be confused with trust. Forgiveness is given freely. Trust is earned over time. You can forgive a person fully and still not yet trust them with the deeper places of your life, because trust is built on a track record that forgiveness does not require. The same applies to you. You can forgive yourself fully today and still walk through a season of rebuilding trust with yourself, by keeping the small promises you make, by showing up for yourself in the daily choices that prove you are now the woman you say you are. The forgiveness is the doorway. The trust is the long faithful walking that follows. Both are required, and they must not be confused.

Part 6

The Father’s verdict.

The Word of God names with stunning clarity how completely the Father has dealt with the failures the accuser keeps trying to bring back to the surface of her life.

"As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us."

Psalm 103:12 NIV

The east and the west do not meet. There is no point on the map where the eastern hemisphere becomes the western one. The distance between them is infinite by design. This is the picture David gives for how far the Father has placed the failures of His daughter from her own person. The transgressions are no longer attached to her. They are not in a folder He is keeping for later. They are not in a file the accuser can subpoena. They have been removed, and the removal is permanent.

And yet many daughters of God spend years revisiting failures that the Father Himself has put away. They keep digging up what He has already buried. They keep prosecuting what He has already pardoned. They live in a constant inner courtroom while the Father stands outside the courtroom door, saying, “Daughter, come out. There is no trial. I closed the case before you ever opened it.”

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come, the old has gone, the new is here."

2 Corinthians 5:17 NIV

Paul names the same truth in a different image. The woman you used to be is not the woman you are now. The new creation has already come. The old has already gone. The work of self-forgiveness is the work of catching up your own inner narrative with what the Lord has already declared about you in the spiritual realm. The new is here. She is the one whose voice you are meant to be living in. The old woman is the one the accuser keeps trying to drag back to the front of your life, but she has no current standing, and you do not owe her another day of your attention.

Part 7

The two letters.

There is a practice the Lord gave me, in a particularly heavy season of self-forgiveness, that I am giving to you now, because it is the most concrete tool I have found for moving the work of this episode out of the realm of theory and into the realm of practiced integration. The practice is the writing of two letters.

The first letter is from your current self to the woman who stayed. The woman who allowed what she allowed. The woman who did not yet see what she now sees. You do not write to scold her. You write to see her. You write to her with the eyes of a wise older woman who has been to the other side of what she is still living through, and who knows exactly what she is doing and exactly why.

Letter one. To the woman who stayed.

I see you, and I see why you stayed. You were not stupid, you were loyal, you were hopeful, you were loving, and you were doing the best you could with what you knew at the time. What you knew at the time was not what you know now.

I see what you hoped for. I see what you believed could still happen. I see the way you kept showing up, kept giving, kept praying, kept waiting, because you believed love could be redemptive, and that belief was not foolish. It was the truest thing about you.

The truth is, you were placed in a situation that was never going to give back what you were giving. Not because of you. Because of them. The container could not hold what you were pouring. The failure was the container, not the pour.

I forgive you, I release you, and I lay down every judgment I have been carrying against you. You did not deserve what happened to you, and you do not deserve to keep being punished for it by the woman you have become. You are safe with me now, you are loved, you are seen, and you are free.

The second letter is from your future healed self, looking back at the woman you are right now, in this exact season of your life. She is on the other side of what you are still learning. She has lived through everything you are about to live through. And she has come back to tell you what she learned, who you become, and how it all turned out.

Letter two. From your future healed self.

I am writing to you from the other side of this season, and I need you to hear this. You make it. Not just make it. You become someone you did not know you could become. You walk in a peace you did not yet know was available to you.

You learn to discern what is real love and what is not. You learn to give from fullness and not from emptiness. You learn to receive what the Father has been holding out to you for years. You learn that your wholeness was never dependent on anyone else’s recognition of it.

The pain you are in right now becomes the very wisdom that helps other women out of theirs. Nothing you are walking through is wasted. The Lord uses every piece of it, and the woman you become carries a depth that no untouched woman could have offered the world.

I love you, and I am proud of you. Keep walking, because the other side is closer than you think.

The letters are not for anyone else to read. They are written by you, to the parts of yourself that have not yet been spoken to with tenderness. You write them slowly, you read them out loud, and you let them sink into the places the accuser has been speaking against for years. They become the new vocabulary the inner woman finally hears, and the new vocabulary is the doorway through which self-forgiveness finally walks in.

Part 8

The beliefs you plant about yourself.

Once the work of forgiveness has begun and the new vocabulary has been received, there is one more piece of this teaching the daughter of God needs to understand, because what she does in the weeks and months that follow will determine whether the forgiveness sets into her as a new way of being or fades back into the old patterns. The teaching is about the nature of belief, and Jesus spoke about belief using an image that should give every woman of God real courage as she begins.

"Truly I tell you, if you have faith as small as a mustard seed, you can say to this mountain, ‘Move from here to there,’ and it will move. Nothing will be impossible for you."

Matthew 17:20 NIV

The mustard seed is one of the smallest seeds in the world. The Lord chose it on purpose, because He wanted His daughters to understand that the size of the belief at the moment of planting is not what determines what the belief becomes. The belief becomes what it was designed to become regardless of how small it looked when it went into the ground. A mustard seed planted in good soil grows into a tree that reaches several meters in just a few months, and a tree at full height is impossible to mistake for the seed it began as.

The same is true of the beliefs you plant about yourself today. You are not required to feel the forgiveness fully the moment you extend it. You are not required to believe the new declarations the first time you say them. You are only required to plant the seed of belief, and to keep watering it through the daily practices of returning to it, and the belief will grow into the very tree the Lord designed it to become. The woman who has forgiven herself today, even if the forgiveness feels small and uncertain, has planted a seed that will become a tree, given enough days of faithful return.

And here is the other thing about a tree that is rooted deeply in good soil. The wind and the weather and the storms that pass through do not change what the tree is becoming. A tree that has put its roots down does not get pulled up by the seasons that pass over it. The outside factors lose their authority once the inside has rooted, and the tree grows according to its design, regardless of what is happening in the world around it. This is the kind of stability the Lord is forming in you. The forgiveness becomes a tree. The tree becomes the new identity. The new identity becomes unshakeable, not because nothing tries to shake it, but because the roots have gone deep enough that the shaking has nothing to grip.

Plant the seed today, even if it is small. Keep watering it through the daily reading of the declarations, through the daily refusal of the accuser’s voice, through the daily receiving of what the Father has been holding out to you for years. The tree is coming. The shade of it is coming. And the rest of your life is meant to be lived under the canopy of the very forgiveness you are planting today.

Part 9

Three movements for this week.

Three movements to walk into this week. Together they form the practical work of moving the teaching of this episode from understanding into lived reality.

Movement one

Write the two letters.

Set aside one quiet hour this week, alone with the Lord. Write the letter from your current self to the woman who stayed. Write it slowly, with tenderness, without rushing. Then write the letter from your future healed self to the woman you are now. Let yourself feel both letters as you write them. Read them out loud once you have finished, and keep them somewhere you can return to in the weeks ahead.

The writing is the work. The reading is the integration. You do not have to share the letters with anyone. They are for the inner woman who has been waiting for someone to speak to her with this kind of tenderness, and the someone is finally going to be you.

Movement two

Name the accuser. Refuse the agreement.

For one full week, pay attention to the accusing voice that has been speaking inside you. Every time it surfaces, every time you catch yourself saying internally “I was so stupid” or “Why did I do that” or “I should have known better,” pause and name the voice for what it is. It is the accuser, not your own voice and not the Father’s voice, and it has no standing.

And then, out loud if you can, answer it. “I was not stupid. I was loyal. I was hopeful. I was loving. I did not deserve what happened, and I am no longer agreeing with what you are saying about me.” Refuse the agreement. The accuser only has power as long as you keep agreeing with him. Withdraw your agreement, and his voice loses its authority.

Movement three

Let the martyr die.

This week, sit with the harder question. Where have you been benefiting from being the one who gives more than she receives? Where have you been holding the moral high ground as a kind of identity? Where has the role of the wronged woman been giving you something that you have not yet been willing to release?

Bring this to the Lord, not to punish yourself, but to release the part of you that has been depending on the martyr identity for emotional satisfaction. Let her die. Thank her for getting you this far. And then ask the Lord for the new identity He is forming in her place, the woman who knows she is the prize, who receives as fully as she gives, and who no longer needs to be wronged in order to know that she is good.

A brief note

On my own walk.

There was a long season in my life when I could not look at myself in the mirror. I would walk past my reflection and avert my eyes, because what I saw in the glass was a woman I had been judging for years. I hated who I had become. I hated the choices I had made. I hated the seasons I had spent in self-destructive habits that no one else could see, and the hating was my way of trying to atone for what I felt I had failed at.

The Lord met me in that mirror, in a season when I had finally exhausted my own ability to punish myself for my past. He showed me that I had been holding a verdict against myself that He had never agreed with. He showed me that the version of myself I was prosecuting was a woman He had already forgiven, already released, already invited into the new life He had been holding out to me all along.

The forgiveness of myself was harder than the forgiveness of anyone who had ever hurt me. It took years. It is still, in some places, ongoing. But the freedom on the other side of that work is unlike anything else I have ever walked into. The woman who has been forgiven by herself walks differently. She receives differently. She loves differently. She no longer needs to perform her way into being worthy, because she has finally accepted the worthiness the Father placed in her from the beginning.

I am giving you the same forgiveness today. Not because I have any authority to give it. But because the Father has been waiting on you to receive it from yourself, and the receiving begins the moment you finally decide that the woman in the mirror is no longer your enemy.

Five questions for this week

Read one a day. Sit with it. Bring it to God in prayer.

One. Of the five common judgments named in Part 1, which one have you been carrying against yourself for the longest, and what would it cost you to lay it down today?

Two. When you look in the mirror, which of the two voices have you been agreeing with most often? What changes inside you when you choose to agree with the Father’s voice instead?

Three. Where have you been calling yourself stupid for being loyal, hopeful, or loving? What is the Lord saying about those very qualities He placed in you on purpose?

Four. Have you been holding a sweetness in the martyr identity that has been keeping you stuck? What would it look like to release it and walk into reciprocity instead?

Five. If you write the two letters this week, which one do you most need to read again the next time the accuser tries to return?

A prayer for the woman forgiving herself

Father, I come to You today not as a stranger to forgiveness, because I have forgiven so many others, but as a stranger to receiving forgiveness for myself. I confess that I have been holding a verdict against the woman I used to be that You never agreed with, and the verdict has been quietly destroying my peace for years.

Today I lay it down. I lay down the judgment I have been carrying against the version of me who stayed too long, gave too much, did not see the signs, and numbed what she could not yet feel. I receive the verdict You have already spoken over me, where there is no condemnation, no transgression held against me, and where the old has gone and the new is here.

Help me to let the martyr die. Help me to release the cleanness of being the wronged one. Help me to walk into the reciprocity You are forming in me, where I give as freely as I receive and receive as freely as I give. Heal the woman in the mirror, until she becomes the woman I am at peace with.

In the name of Jesus, amen.

Before you go

One practice for this week.

Stand in front of a mirror this week. Just once. Look yourself in the eye, the way you would look into the eyes of a daughter you love. And say, out loud, slowly enough that your spirit hears it:

I forgive you. I see why you stayed. You were loyal. You were hopeful. You were loving. You did not deserve what happened. I lay down every judgment. You are free.

Then breathe. Let her receive it. The woman in the mirror has been waiting for this, longer than you have any idea. Today is the day. The forgiveness she has been refusing herself for years is finally arriving, from the only person whose forgiveness could ever heal her, which is you.

The hardest forgiveness
