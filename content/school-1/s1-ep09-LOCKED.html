School 1: The abandoned girl  ·  Episode 9

When love becomes a weapon.

A teaching on the counterfeit affection that has been costing you yourself, and the love God designed that finally protects who you are.

Welcome

Daughter of Zion, we come now to a teaching that names a dynamic so common across women's lives that almost every one of us has lived inside some version of it, and yet so quietly disguised that almost none of us have had the language to name what was happening to us while it was happening.

Love is one of the most powerful forces in the universe, and God Himself is named love by the Word, not as a description of His feelings but as a description of His very being. Anything as powerful as love can, in the hands of a wounded person, be used not to build but to break, and that is the dynamic this episode names. The same words and gestures and intimacies that were designed to make a woman more of herself can be used, in a wrong relationship, to make her less of herself, and the cruelest part of the dynamic is that the very vocabulary of love is what is being used to wound her, so she cannot even recognize the wounding for what it is.

This is one of the most important episodes in this school, because almost every other teaching in this program will not hold if the woman in front of me is currently inside a dynamic where love has been weaponized against her. She can do all the inner work in the world, but if she returns each night to a person who is using affection as a tool of control, the work will not stick, because the work cannot heal what continues to wound her in the very place it is trying to heal.

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It does not dishonor others, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres."

1 Corinthians 13:4-7 NIV

This is the diagnostic. Paul is not writing a sentimental verse for wedding ceremonies, he is writing the most rigorous definition of love the Word of God offers, and every line is meant to be used as a measuring stick against any relationship that calls itself love. Patient. Kind. Not envious. Not proud. Not dishonoring. Not self-seeking. Not easily angered. Keeping no record of wrongs. Protecting. Trusting. Hoping. Persevering. If what has been called love in your life has not looked like this, the Word of God is naming clearly that it was not love. It was something else wearing the costume of love, and the discernment between the two is the work of this entire episode.

Part 1

What weaponized love actually is.

When we say love has been weaponized, we mean that the language and gestures of love are being used not to build the woman up but to control, punish, or diminish her. The wounding part is not the absence of affection. The wounding part is that the affection itself has become the tool. A man who simply ignored her would be easier to leave than a man who tells her he loves her in the same breath he uses to tear her apart, because the affection is the very thing keeping her loyal to the wound.

Weaponized love confuses the nervous system in a way that no other form of harm can confuse it, because the body cannot file what is happening into a single category. Half of the body is registering love and saying stay. The other half is registering harm and saying flee. The conflict between these two signals creates a kind of paralysis, and the paralysis is often interpreted by the woman herself as her own failure to leave, when really it is her body responding accurately to a situation that has been designed, intentionally or not, to keep her trapped between two truths.

The person using love as a weapon is not always doing it consciously. Many wounded people learned this language from their own families of origin, and they are repeating, often without awareness, what was done to them when they were small. This does not make the harm less real, but it does explain why simple confrontation rarely changes the pattern. The pattern is older than this relationship, it is the very water the wounded person has been swimming in for their whole life, and they cannot see the water any more than a fish can see the ocean it lives inside of.

The work for you, as the woman of God, is not to fix the wounded person doing the wounding. That is between them and the Lord. The work is to recognize what is happening, name it clearly, and refuse to let what has been called love continue to define your understanding of what love actually is. The recognition is the beginning of the freedom.

Part 2

The four-stage cycle.

One of the most important things to understand about weaponized love is that it almost always runs in a predictable cycle, and once you can see the cycle clearly, you can never again be confused by the moments of warmth that follow the moments of harm. The cycle is mathematical. It has stages. And the stages repeat themselves until the woman who is inside the cycle finds the courage to step out.

1

Idealization.

He adores you. He mirrors your faith. He says all the right words. He tells you he has never met a woman like you before, and you believe him, because the attention is so intense and so specific that it could not possibly be a script. The relationship feels destined.

2

Devaluation.

He starts to find fault. He mocks the things he once admired. He twists your goodness into accusations of manipulation or games. The woman he was praising last month is now the woman he is criticizing this month, and you do not understand what changed, because you have not changed.

3

Discard or threat.

He belittles or cuts you down. He tells you you are a waste of his time. He threatens to end the relationship, or actually ends it temporarily. The threats can take many forms. “You are lucky I put up with you.” “I do not know how much more of this I can take.” “Maybe I should just go.” “You are pushing me away.” Each one is a different way of using the fear of losing him to control you.

4

Hoovering.

He returns. He softens. He says, “I was angry, I am sorry, I am worried about you, you mean everything to me.” The version of him from the idealization stage briefly reappears, and the relief floods your body, and you are pulled right back in.

Each time you re-engage, the abusive part gets bolder and the tender part gets shorter. That is the mathematical truth of this dynamic. Every cycle costs you more peace and takes longer to recover from, and the idealization phases get briefer with each iteration while the devaluation phases get longer. The cycle is not love and harm in equal measure. It is harm being slowly normalized through occasional moments of relief, and the relief is what your nervous system has been mistaking for love.

Real love does not cycle. Real love does not require recovery periods. Real love does not have an idealization phase, because real love is consistent, and consistency does not need to manufacture intensity through periodic withdrawal. The kind of love the Lord designed for you is the same person, day after day, year after year, with no sudden swings between adoration and contempt. Anything else is a counterfeit, no matter how convincing the warm phases are.

Part 3

The six shapes.

Beyond the cycle itself, weaponized love comes in particular shapes that the woman of God needs to be able to recognize on sight. The shapes are not always all present in one relationship, but if even one or two are consistently present, the principle applies.

The first shape is criticism disguised as care. The words sound like guidance. “I am just trying to help you grow. I am saying this because I love you. Someone has to be honest with you.” But the cumulative effect is that nothing about her is ever quite right. Her body, her work, her voice, her hair, her cooking, her parenting, her clothes, her opinions, her friends, all become things that need correcting. Real correction lifts a person up. Weaponized correction cuts a person down while wearing the face of help, and the woman who has been receiving it for years often does not realize she has been slowly trained to find herself inadequate at every turn.

The second shape is the silent treatment used as punishment. When she does something he does not like, he withdraws his presence, his affection, his communication, sometimes for hours and sometimes for days. The withdrawal is unspoken and unexplained, and she is left to figure out what she did wrong and to perform whatever apology will restore the connection. This is not the natural distance that grown adults sometimes need to process a hard conversation, this is the deliberate use of his absence as a tool to teach her what behavior will cost her his love.

The third shape is conditional love. The affection appears when she pleases him and disappears when she does not. The relationship is structured as a kind of ongoing audition, where she has to keep earning the love each day, and the bar moves whenever it suits him. Real love is not earned by performance, real love is a covenant, where the giver does not retract what he has given simply because she has displeased him in a particular moment.

The fourth shape is the contrast between public love and private cruelty. He is wonderful with her in front of other people, attentive, complimentary, generous, the kind of partner her friends envy. And then they get home, and the warmth disappears, and the criticism returns, and she is left wondering whether she is making the private version up, because the public version was so convincing. The dissonance is exhausting, and it isolates her, because no one in her life believes what she is living through could possibly be happening when the version they see is so different.

The fifth shape is surveillance disguised as concern. He checks where she has been, who she talked to, what she wore, where her phone is. The language always sounds protective. “I just want to know you are safe.” “I am only asking because I care.” “I worry when I do not hear from you.” “A man who loves a woman wants to know what she is doing.” Each sentence sounds caring on the surface, but the function is possessive. Healthy love says, “I hope you are okay.” Weaponized love says, in a hundred different sentences, “I need to know what you are doing so I do not lose control of you.” The difference is everything.

The sixth shape is spiritual manipulation. He uses God, Scripture, and the language of faith to shame her, to question her boundaries, to make her doubt herself. Real spiritual connection draws her closer to peace and discernment, never confusion or guilt. When a man mixes Scripture with arrogance or mockery, that is not spiritual leadership, it is spiritual warfare wearing the costume of headship, and the woman of God must learn to recognize it the moment it appears.

Each of these is the same dynamic at the root. Love being used as a tool to keep her smaller than she was designed to be, instead of love functioning as the very thing that grows her into who God formed her to be.

And remember, as we named in the trauma bond episode, these patterns do not appear only in romantic relationships. A mother can weaponize love against an adult daughter. A friend can weaponize love against a friend. A pastor or mentor can weaponize spiritual care into spiritual control. The dynamic transcends the relationship category. Wherever the affection is being used as a tool of diminishment, the teaching of this episode applies.

The inversion

If you are afraid, you are not in love.

Darling, the inversion at the heart of this teaching is one of the clearest diagnostics the Word of God gives a woman about what is real and what is counterfeit in the relationships of her life.

If you are afraid of him, you are not in love with him. You are in survival. Real love produces peace and safety, not vigilance and chronic fear.

"There is no fear in love. But perfect love drives out fear, because fear has to do with punishment. The one who fears is not made perfect in love."

1 John 4:18 NIV

John is naming a truth so foundational that almost every confused relationship dissolves the moment a woman applies it honestly. Real love and fear cannot coexist. Where love is real and mature and rightly held, fear is driven out, because the love itself produces a security that fear cannot survive in. If you are constantly anxious in the presence of someone who claims to love you, the love is either immature or absent. If you are walking on eggshells around someone who claims to love you, what you are calling love is something else.

Your body has been telling you the truth all along. The vigilance you feel before he gets home from work is not your problem. The tightness in your chest when you hear his voice on the phone is not anxiety you need to manage. The relief you feel when he is not in the room is not a sign of your immaturity. These are diagnostic signals your nervous system has been sending you for months or years, and the work of this episode is to finally listen to them instead of overriding them in the name of trying harder.

Ask yourself one honest question. When do you feel God’s presence stronger, when you are near him, or when you are away from him? Which version of you does he awaken, the calm, radiant one, or the anxious, apologetic one? Your peace is evidence of truth. The Lord does not send His daughter into relationships that suffocate her spirit. If your spirit goes quiet around someone, that is information, and you are meant to listen to it.

Part 4

The wisdom of Proverbs.

The Word of God provides an even sharper diagnostic for distinguishing true love from its counterfeit, and the diagnostic is so counterintuitive that many women miss it entirely.

"Better is open rebuke than hidden love. Wounds from a friend can be trusted, but an enemy multiplies kisses."

Proverbs 27:5-6 NIV

This is one of the most important diagnostics in all of Scripture for the woman trying to discern what is real love and what is its imitation. Solomon is naming that the warmth of an exterior is not the measure of love. The fruit is the measure. A friend who tells you a hard truth out of genuine care for you is loving you. An enemy who showers you with affection while quietly working against you is harming you, and the affection itself is the very camouflage of the harm.

The second line of that verse is profound. Solomon is naming that the multiplication of affectionate gestures, the constant declarations of love, the over-the-top compliments and gifts and attentions, can sometimes be the very evidence of an enemy at work, not a friend. The friend speaks the hard word. The friend offers the open rebuke. The friend trusts the relationship enough to tell the truth that costs something to say. The enemy hides behind the gestures and uses them as cover for the slow undoing of the woman they are claiming to love.

The principle here is that the surface of a relationship is never the measure of its truth. The fruit is. The Lord said it Himself. “By their fruit you will recognize them.” The fruit of real love is a more whole woman, a more confident woman, a more peaceful woman, a more freely expressing woman. The fruit of weaponized love, no matter how affectionate the surface, is a smaller woman, an anxious woman, a self-doubting woman, a woman who used to be more of herself than she is now.

If the fruit of the relationship is the diminishment of who you were when it began, the relationship is not loving you, regardless of how warm its surface has been.

The principle

The measure of love is its fruit, not its surface. Real love makes you more of yourself. Weaponized love makes you less. Apply the diagnostic to every relationship of your life, and listen to what the fruit tells you about what has actually been happening in the soil.

Part 5

Altruism is not holiness.

There is a teaching that has to be added to this episode, because the woman of God who has been receiving weaponized love has often been receiving it specifically because she has been operating from a misplaced sense of virtue that the world and sometimes even the church have been calling holiness.

Altruism is not holiness. Being so selfless that you allow others to use and abuse you is not an act of care, for you cannot possibly care for others if you do not care for yourself. When you are so kind to everyone that you allow anyone to walk over you, you are simply disrespecting yourself, and there is nothing noble about altruism that costs you your dignity.

Altruism is in fact the mirror image of narcissism. The narcissist hides his low self-esteem behind a wall of self-defensive grandiosity. The altruist hides her low self-esteem behind a wall of self-defensive generosity. Both are operating from the same root, which is a hidden sense of unworthiness, and both produce the same outcome, which is relationships that drain instead of build. The narcissist takes because he believes he deserves to. The altruist gives until she is empty because she does not yet believe she deserves to receive.

When a daughter of God allows herself to be abused, disrespected, and mistreated in the name of being patient or loving or long-suffering, she is not helping anyone. She is allowing the spirits behind the abuse to grow, multiply, and spread onto even more people around them. The very thing she thinks she is doing for love is reinforcing the very pattern that is wounding her and that will go on to wound others through her continued silence. The kindest thing you can do for a wounded person is refuse to let them wound you any further, because the wounding is not just hurting you, it is reinforcing the spirit that is keeping them in their own bondage.

Selflessness does not always mean caring. Taking care of yourself first is one of the highest forms of taking care of you and of the people God has called you to love well. You cannot give what you do not have. If you have not been receiving love, you cannot give it from a sustainable place. If you have not been honored, you do not yet know how to teach the people around you how to honor you. The work of receiving what God has for you is not selfish, it is the prerequisite for everything else He is asking you to give.

Part 6

The unequally yoked heart.

One of the deepest reasons weaponized love produces such confusion in a daughter of God is that the relationship has often been unequally yoked from the beginning, and the woman did not have the language for what was wrong, only the constant sense that something was wrong.

"Do not be yoked together with unbelievers. For what do righteousness and wickedness have in common? Or what fellowship can light have with darkness?"

2 Corinthians 6:14 NIV

Paul is naming a principle that goes far beyond shared religious affiliation. He is naming that two people cannot pull the same plow in opposite directions and expect the field to be worked. When one person is operating from humility, forgiveness, and grace, and the other is operating from ego, control, or self-reliance, the relationship is no longer about resolution, it is about dominance. Her heart wanted partnership. His heart wanted power. And every time she tried to speak with softness or with faith, it bounced off the stone of his orientation toward control.

The lesson is not never trust again, the lesson is trust your spirit’s first discomfort. You likely sensed early on that the spiritual alignment was not there, but you overrode the sense because you wanted to believe the best. The Holy Spirit does not confuse or condemn, He simply withdraws when something is not right, and the withdrawal of His peace is one of the clearest signals the daughter of God has access to about whether a relationship is for her or against her. Next time, let that unease guide you, and let it be the voice you trust before the voice of any man.

What is required now is not punishment but realignment. Repentance is just turning back toward what is holy, not punishing yourself for what is past. God’s correction always comes with mercy. He is not angry, He is inviting you closer. The next relationship He gives you must share your faith, not in label but in fruit, in kindness, humility, prayer and forgiveness. Anything less is unequally yoked, no matter what title the relationship wears.

Part 7

What real love looks like.

The clearest way to recognize what weaponized love is, is to know what real love is, so clearly and so deeply that the counterfeit becomes obvious by contrast.

Real love is reciprocal care, not one person sacrificing themselves while the other receives. The flow goes both ways. He gives and you give and the giving feels mutual, and the result is two people both being built up by what is passing between them, not one person being slowly drained while the other is being fed.

Real love is safety and protection, not threats and chronic fear. You feel calmer in his presence, not more vigilant. Your nervous system can rest around him. He is not a source of unpredictable danger but a source of steady warmth, and the steadiness becomes the very foundation on which the relationship is built.

Real love is studying you, not just taking from you. He is curious about who you are, he pays attention to what you say, he remembers what matters to you. He has been actively learning the specific woman God placed in front of him, and his learning is the evidence that he is genuinely engaged with the soul of you, not just the function you serve in his life.

Real love brings the best out of you. The version of you that emerges in the relationship is more confident, more free, more expressive, more alive than the version of you that walked into the relationship. The relationship is the soil in which you are growing, not the box you are being kept small inside of.

Real love is patient with your becoming. It does not demand that you arrive at some imaginary finished state before it deems you worthy of being loved. It walks with you through your unfinished places, holds the unfinished places with tenderness, and trusts the Lord to keep doing in you what only He can do.

And real love is built on earned intimacy, not on instant vulnerability. Your softness is a privilege, not a test. Give it only to those who show emotional consistency over time, and watch how a person handles small conflicts before you offer them the deepest parts of you. The person who responds with gentleness in the small things will respond with gentleness in the large ones. The person who twists your words in the small things will twist them in the large ones. Do not give your softness to a man who has not yet proven he can hold it without breaking it.

Part 8

Three movements for this week.

Three movements to walk into this week. Together they form the practice of discernment and the beginning of refusing to be diminished any further by what has been called love but has not actually been love.

Movement one

Apply the 1 Corinthians 13 diagnostic.

Take a quiet hour this week and write out the lines of 1 Corinthians 13 down the left side of a page. Patient. Kind. Not envious. Not boastful. Not proud. Not dishonoring. Not self-seeking. Not easily angered. Keeps no record of wrongs. Protects. Trusts. Hopes. Perseveres. Then beside each line, write honestly whether the relationship you are evaluating fits or does not fit that line in actual daily practice over the last twelve months. Not in the best moments. In daily practice.

Do not soften the evaluation. The Word of God did not soften the definition, and you cannot afford to soften the application. Whatever the page shows you, sit with it in the presence of the Lord and ask Him what He is asking you to do with what He has just shown you. The honest naming is the beginning of the freedom.

Movement two

Listen to the body.

For one week, pay attention to the signals your body has been sending you about the people in your life. When you are about to be in their presence, notice what your shoulders do. When you hear their voice, notice what happens in your chest. When you anticipate seeing them, notice what your breath does. The body has been keeping the score of every interaction, and the body has been telling you the truth about whether the relationship is safe or whether it has been costing you your peace.

Bring those signals to the Lord and ask Him to help you read them honestly. The vigilance is information. The tightness is information. The relief at their absence is information. None of this is a moral failure on your part. It is your nervous system telling you what your conscious mind has been overriding, and the work of this week is to finally listen.

Movement three

Stop financing what is not feeding you.

The principle of this third movement is so simple and so freeing that many women do not believe it could be the answer to as much as it is the answer to. Stop investing your emotional, spiritual and even financial resources into the relationship that has not been giving back. The pouring is what has been keeping the cycle going. The moment you stop pouring, the dynamic loses the fuel that has been keeping it running for years.

You do not have to make any dramatic declarations. You do not have to deliver any speeches. You only have to begin, quietly, to withdraw the giving that has been propping up the dynamic. Stop overexplaining yourself. Stop chasing his approval. Stop performing for his moods. Stop financing what he is too disengaged to invest in himself. The slow withdrawal of your emotional fuel is the slow unwinding of the bond, and the unwinding is what the Lord has been waiting for you to begin.

A brief note

On my own walk.

I have lived inside the dynamic this episode names, in more than one relationship and in more than one season of my life. I learned, the long way, the difference between a love that builds and a love that takes. I learned that the warm phases were the camouflage, not the evidence. I learned that the cycle was not just exhausting, it was reshaping me into a smaller version of myself with each iteration, and that the woman I was becoming inside the cycle was not the woman God had formed me to be.

What broke the pattern for me was not a confrontation with the person doing the wounding. The break came when I finally trusted what my body had been telling me for years, brought what I was sensing to the Lord, and let Him show me the truth my own heart had been trying to override in the name of being patient and forgiving and long-suffering. The Lord showed me clearly that long-suffering toward someone who is consistently harming me is not the same as the long-suffering He commends in His Word, and that the love He had for me was not asking me to keep being broken so that someone else could keep being comfortable.

Love did not abandon me. Someone who could not match it did. That is the line that finally set me free, and it is the line I am giving to you now, because the daughter of God who has been carrying the false belief that love itself has failed her needs to know that love is not the problem. Counterfeit love is the problem, and counterfeit love is not love at all. The real love is still on the other side of this teaching, and the Lord is the only One who can give it to you in its true form.

Five questions for this week

Read one a day. Sit with it. Bring it to God in prayer.

One. When you measure the relationship in front of you against 1 Corinthians 13, where does it match and where does it fail? What is the honest evaluation telling you that you have been refusing to hear?

Two. Which of the six shapes of weaponized love do you recognize most in your own life, and how has the recognition of it begun to shift how you understand what has been happening?

Three. When do you feel God’s presence stronger, when you are near this person, or when you are away from them? What is that signal telling you that you have been afraid to act on?

Four. Where has altruism been disguising itself as holiness in your life, and what would it look like to receive love before you continue giving it?

Five. What is the one place this week that you can begin to stop financing what is not feeding you, and trust the Lord with what happens when you stop?

A prayer for the daughter walking out of weaponized love

Father, I come to You with a heart that has been confused for a long time about what love is, because what has been called love in my life has not always been love.

I confess that I have been overriding the signals You placed in my body to keep me safe. I confess that I have been calling altruism holiness and patience weakness. I confess that I have been pouring into people who could not pour back, and calling the depletion devotion.

Open my eyes. Show me clearly what I have been seeing dimly. Give me the discernment of Proverbs and the clarity of 1 Corinthians 13. Teach me to read the fruit instead of the surface, and to trust the peace of Your Spirit over the intensity of any feeling I have learned to call love.

I release the relationships You are asking me to release, and I receive the love You are preparing for me in their place. Make me a woman who knows the difference between counterfeit and covenant, and let the rest of my life be lived inside the kind of love You designed for me from the beginning.

In the name of Jesus, amen.

Before you go

One question, honestly answered.

This week, just one practice. Ask yourself one honest question and answer it without softening, without explaining, without protecting anyone, including yourself.

Which version of me does this relationship awaken? The calm and radiant one, or the anxious and apologetic one?

The answer is your data. The data is your discernment. The discernment is the doorway out of every dynamic that has been quietly diminishing you for years. You do not have to take any dramatic action this week, you only have to know the truth. The knowing is what unlocks every step that comes after.

The Father who loves you is not asking you to stay in what is killing you. He is calling you out, gently, patiently, into a love He paid the highest price to give you. Counterfeit love made you small. Real love will make you whole. And the moment you stop accepting the counterfeit, the real begins to find its way to you.

Love did not abandon you.
Someone who could not match it did.

still exists.
And it is the only kind that will ever make you whole.
