School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 7

How abandonment wrecks your relationships

The healthiest relationship you will ever have is the one you are building with yourself right now. Everything else flows from there.

"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."

Proverbs 4:23 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

You bring yourself into every relationship you enter. And if you have not yet healed the wound, you bring the wound too. Not because you are broken. Because the wound has not yet been separated from your identity.

I want to look at the specific ways the abandonment wound shows up in relationships. Not to shame you. To name what is happening so that you can stop being confused by it and start healing it.

Part 1

You test people without knowing you are doing it

Unconsciously, you create situations that reveal whether someone will stay. You push a little to see if they push back. You go quiet to see if they notice. You need too much or too little to see how they respond.

You are not doing this consciously. You are doing this because your nervous system learned very early that people leave, and it needs to know whether this person is going to be one of them.

The problem is that the testing is exhausting for both of you, and it often creates the very abandonment it was designed to prevent. The person feels they can never win, and eventually they stop trying. And your wound says: see. I knew it.

"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."

Proverbs 4:23 NIV
Part 2

You confuse intensity with intimacy

If a relationship is calm and consistent, something inside you says it is boring, or that they do not really love you, because the love you learned early was unpredictable. Hot and cold, present and absent. Your nervous system learned to read the chaos as passion and the stillness as indifference.

Real intimacy is built in the quiet, in the consistency, in the showing up when it is not dramatic, in the conversation at the kitchen table on a Wednesday night.

But if your template was built on chaos, quiet feels like distance. And you will keep chasing the fire without realising you are running from the very thing you actually need.

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud."

1 Corinthians 13:4 NIV
Part 3

You give from fear rather than from love

You give endlessly. You over-extend yourself, you say yes when you mean no and you prioritise everyone else. And you tell yourself this is love.

But underneath the giving, if you are honest, there is a fear. The fear that if you stop giving, they will stop staying. The giving is not generosity. It is insurance.

You are not too much. You are too giving. And there is a difference that will change everything once you understand it.

Real love gives from overflow, not from the desperate need to earn your place. The difference between the two is not visible from the outside, but you can feel it from the inside. The giver who gives from fear is always depleted. The giver who gives from fullness is always replenished. The source matters.

Part 4

The healing is in the relationship with yourself first

You cannot heal your relationship patterns by finding the right person. You can only heal them by doing the inner work that changes what you bring to every person you encounter.

And that work is this: learning to stay with yourself, to not abandon yourself when things get hard, to choose your peace over someone else's approval, consistently, until your nervous system learns that you are safe.

"I will never leave you nor forsake you."

Hebrews 13:5 NIV

This promise is not just about God's relationship with you. It is the model for your relationship with yourself. I will not forsake me. I will not abandon me when I am inconvenient. I will not shrink me to make someone else comfortable.

That is the covenant you are making with yourself in this healing. And it is the covenant that transforms every relationship that comes after it.

Questions to sit with this week

One. Where do you see yourself testing people in your relationships right now?

Two. What does calm, consistent love feel like to you? Does it attract you or does something in you pull away from it?

Three. What are you most afraid would happen if you stopped over-giving in your closest relationships?

Four. Where are you still giving from fear rather than from genuine overflow?

Five. What would it look like to love from a full place rather than an empty one?

A prayer for this episode

God, show me where my wounds are showing up in my relationships.

Show me where I am testing instead of trusting. Chasing chaos instead of resting in calm. Giving from fear instead of from overflow.

Heal the patterns. Not just the surface behaviours. The root beliefs underneath them.

Teach me to stay with myself so I can stop needing other people to stay for me in order to feel safe.

Amen.
