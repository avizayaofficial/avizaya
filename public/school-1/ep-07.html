School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 7

How abandonment wrecks your relationships

The healthiest relationship you will ever have is the one you are building with yourself right now. Everything else flows from there.

"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."

Proverbs 4:23 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

You bring yourself into every relationship you enter. And if you have not yet healed the wound, you bring the wound too. Not because you are broken. Because the wound has not yet been separated from your identity.

Maybe you have asked yourself the question so many of us have asked, often with confusion and sometimes with despair. Why do I keep ending up with the same kind of love, no matter how many times I promise myself this one will be different? The faces change, the names change, the seasons of life change, and yet the dynamic underneath turns out, months or years in, to be the same dynamic you have lived through before, with the same disappointments, the same misunderstandings and the same loneliness inside what was supposed to be a different kind of love.

The world tells you that you have bad luck. Your friends tell you that you have a type. Your own heart tells you that you must be broken in some way that keeps drawing the same kind of person to you again and again. None of those explanations reach the root, and so none of them can free you from the cycle. Here is what does reach it. The patterns you repeat in love are not random and they are not bad luck. They are the patterns your nervous system was trained to recognise as love before you had any words to question them, and until those patterns are renewed at the level God works at, the level of the mind and the spirit, the cycle will keep finding new faces to play out the same old story through.

So I want to look at the specific ways the abandonment wound shows up in relationships. Not to shame you. To name what is happening so that you can stop being confused by it and start healing it.

Part 1

You test people without knowing you are doing it

Unconsciously, you create situations that reveal whether someone will stay. You push a little to see if they push back. You go quiet to see if they notice. You need too much or too little to see how they respond.

You are not doing this consciously. You are doing this because your nervous system learned very early that people leave, and it needs to know whether this person is going to be one of them. Every test is a question your body is asking on your behalf, the same question it has been asking since you were a girl. Are you going to leave me too?

The problem is that the testing is exhausting for both of you, and it often creates the very abandonment it was designed to prevent. The person feels they can never win, that no amount of staying is ever counted as proof, and eventually they stop trying. And your wound says: see. I knew it.

The wound is not gathering evidence about them. It is manufacturing evidence for a verdict it reached years ago. That is why no relationship can pass a test the wound is grading, because the wound has never once graded fairly in its life.

"Above all else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."

Proverbs 4:23 NIV

Everything you do flows from the heart, including the testing. Which means the testing does not end by trying harder to trust. It ends when the heart it flows from is healed.

Part 2

You confuse intensity with intimacy

If a relationship is calm and consistent, something inside you says it is boring, or that they do not really love you, because the love you learned early was unpredictable. Hot and cold, present and absent. Your nervous system learned to read the chaos as passion and the stillness as indifference.

Here is the principle underneath this, and it is so simple it sounds almost insulting at first. You do not choose what feels good. You choose what feels familiar. Your nervous system was trained, somewhere in the earliest years of your life, to recognise a particular kind of relational energy as love. That training was not a choice. It happened to you, the way the shape of your face happened to you. If the people around you were warm and consistent and present, then warm and consistent and present is what feels like home. But if they were unpredictable, or absent, or critical, or anxious, then those are the shapes your body now files under love, and the person who carries them will feel like the one you have been looking for all along.

This is why a woman can meet a kind, steady, available man and feel almost nothing, while a man carrying the same patterns that hurt her in childhood makes her feel an electric pull she will spend the rest of the year trying to explain. That is not chemistry. That is recognition. Her nervous system has met someone who matches her training, and the relief of being on familiar ground is so strong it gets misread as love at first sight.

And the reverse is just as disorienting. When real love finally appears, it can feel strange at first. It may even feel boring. Peace and consistency do not match what your nervous system has been calling love, and the absence of the old cycle can register, for a long time, as the absence of love itself. This is a test a woman can fail without ever knowing she failed it. The steady, present person comes along. No games, no disappearing and reappearing, no highs and lows for your body to interpret as passion. Just the same warm attention day after day. And she finds herself wondering whether something is missing, tells her friends there is no spark, and walks away from the very love God had been preparing for her, because it did not feel like the love she was used to.

The love she was used to was not love. It was the cycle of her wounds.

I know this pattern from the inside. For years the steady ones felt almost invisible to me, while the unstable ones felt like the love I had been waiting my whole life to find. I did not yet know I was choosing the familiar and calling it chemistry. I did not yet know that the warmth in my body around a particular kind of person was not God confirming anything. It was my wounds recognising the dynamic they had been trained for. Learning to tell the difference changed the whole course of my life, and it will change yours.

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud."

1 Corinthians 13:4 NIV

Real intimacy is built in the quiet, in the consistency, in the showing up when it is not dramatic, in the conversation at the kitchen table on a Wednesday night. Patient and kind is not a low bar. It is the whole standard, and it is quieter than the counterfeit.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV

Paul is naming the mechanism of change. Conformity to the old pattern is the default, because the mind was shaped before you were old enough to consent to the shaping, and that shape is now the lens through which you see every choice you make, especially in love. Transformation is not behavioural. It is mental. The mind has to be renewed before the choices can be renewed, because the choices are the fruit of the mind making them. Vows will not hold. Forcing yourself toward the steady person while your whole body screams for the unsteady one will not hold. But as the Word works on the deep places where the old definitions of love were planted, your feelings begin to follow. You begin to feel safe with the safe person. You begin to feel drawn to the steady one. What used to register as nothing starts to register as peace.

Part 3

You give from fear rather than from love

You give endlessly. You over-extend yourself, you say yes when you mean no and you prioritise everyone else. And you tell yourself this is love.

But underneath the giving, if you are honest, there is a fear. The fear that if you stop giving, they will stop staying. The giving is not generosity. It is insurance.

There is a particular shape of this that deserves its own naming, because the women who carry it are often the last to see it, and the world has been praising them for the very thing keeping them stuck. It is the mask of competence. The woman who learned early that her needs were not going to be met often becomes the woman who specialises in not needing anything. She becomes the most capable person in every room, the friend everyone calls when they are falling apart, the one who never drops a ball, the one who has it all together while quietly carrying alone what would crush someone else. The mask serves her. It earns respect and approval and a self-sufficiency that protects her from the disappointment of needing someone who will not show up.

But the mask has a price, and the price shows up most painfully in love. The people she attracts tend to be one of two kinds. The first admires her competence but never meets her depth. He loves how strong she is and how little she asks of him. What he is loving is the version of her that does not need him, and the longer she stays the lonelier she becomes, because no one is meeting the woman underneath the mask. He does not even know that woman exists. She has been careful never to show him. The second kind is drawn to her competence because he is in some kind of chaos and senses she will hold him through it. She does, for years, and becomes the strong one in a relationship that was supposed to be mutual, until her whole identity is caretaking and she no longer knows who she is without the role.

Both dynamics are the same problem underneath. The mask keeps the real woman invisible, so only people who love the mask keep arriving. Setting the mask down is not weakness. It is wholeness. The strongest woman in the room is not the one who needs nothing. She is the one who knows what she needs and is not afraid to receive it from the right people.

You are not too much. You are too giving. And there is a difference that will change everything once you understand it. Real love gives from overflow, not from the desperate need to earn your place. The difference between the two is not visible from the outside, but you can feel it from the inside. The giver who gives from fear is always depleted. The giver who gives from fullness is always replenished. The source matters.

Part 4

Your heart is not a reliable witness yet

Scripture is more unflinching about the human heart than much modern teaching is willing to be, and you need to hear it before you can walk into the freedom this episode is offering.

"The heart is deceitful above all things and beyond cure. Who can understand it?"

Jeremiah 17:9 NIV

The culture tells you to follow your heart. But the unrenewed heart will deceive a woman about love, and the wounds in her are precisely the places it deceives her most successfully. Your heart tells you this one is different. Your heart tells you the warning signs are not real, that you would know if something were wrong, because you have chemistry and connection and every marker your body has been trained to read as love. In those moments your heart is the very organ that has been compromised by your wounds. It is not a trustworthy witness. It has too much invested. It wants the relief of the familiar, and it will write whatever verdict keeps the cycle going if you let it hold the pen.

This is why you learn to bring your feelings to God and ask Him what is actually happening, instead of asking your own heart for the verdict. He searches the heart. He sees what it is hiding from the woman who carries it. And the same Scripture that warns you not to follow the unrenewed heart also commands you to guard it. Guarding your heart is not coldness. It is wisdom. It is paying attention to what you let in, what you let near and what you let shape you, and refusing to let your wounds make decisions for you by recognising, in real time, when they are trying to.

"We demolish arguments and every pretension that sets itself up against the knowledge of God, and we take captive every thought to make it obedient to Christ."

2 Corinthians 10:5 NIV

This is the daily practice the renewed mind requires. The old thought rises. You do not simply receive it. You take it captive, hold it against what God has said and ask whether it matches. If it matches, you keep it. If it does not, you release it, knowing it was the voice of the cycle and not the voice of the Lord. In the beginning you will do this a hundred times a day. A year in, ten times a day. Eventually almost never, because the renewed mind becomes the default, and the wounded mind becomes something you remember as the way you used to think back when you did not yet know who you were.

Part 5

The healing is in the relationship with yourself first

You cannot heal your relationship patterns by finding the right person. You can only heal them by doing the inner work that changes what you bring to every person you encounter.

And that work is this: learning to stay with yourself, to not abandon yourself when things get hard, to choose your peace over someone else's approval, consistently, until your nervous system learns that you are safe.

"Never will I leave you; never will I forsake you."

Hebrews 13:5 NIV

This promise is not just about God's relationship with you. It is the model for your relationship with yourself. I will not forsake me. I will not abandon me when I am inconvenient. I will not shrink me to make someone else comfortable. That is the covenant you are making with yourself in this healing, and it is the covenant that transforms every relationship that comes after it.

Because when the covenant holds, you finally have a true picture to measure everything else against. Healthy love does not feel like a roller coaster. It feels like ground. It is consistent in small things, the call that comes when they said it would, the energy that is the same today as it was yesterday, the words and the actions that match. There are no dramatic highs because there are no dramatic lows to swing back from, and the absence of the swing is the evidence that the love is real.

Healthy love makes you more yourself, not less. You do not have to shrink to keep it or perform to deserve it. You bring your real self to the table and you are met there, and at the end of the conversation you feel known, not exhausted. Healthy love can disagree without disappearing. You can have a hard conversation and both still be there at the end of it, with no silence used as a weapon and no love withdrawn as a punishment. And healthy love does not need you to be anyone's saviour or anyone's project, because both people are doing their own work and offering each other the overflow of what God has been giving them.

If that kind of love feels strange to you at first, stay in the strangeness. Pray through it. Let your mind keep being renewed, and watch the steadiness become the very thing your soul has been thirsting for all along.

Questions to sit with this week

One. Where do you see yourself testing people in your relationships right now, and what question is the test really asking?

Two. What does calm, consistent love feel like to you? Does it attract you, or does something in you pull away from it and call it boredom?

Three. What is the pattern that has repeated across your relationships, and where did you first learn to recognise that pattern as love?

Four. Have you been wearing a mask of competence that keeps you from being met? What are you most afraid would happen if you set it down and let yourself be seen as a woman who has needs?

Five. Where are you still giving from fear rather than from genuine overflow, and what would it look like to love from a full place rather than an empty one?

A practice: familiarity or peace

Two small pieces of work this week, and neither one requires you to change anything yet. They only require you to see.

First, map the pattern. Take a quiet hour and write out the last three significant relationships in your life. For each one, list what drew you in, the dynamic that developed once you were a few months in, and how it ended or shifted. Then look across all three and ask the honest question. What is the same? The pattern will surface. It always does once it is laid out on paper, and you cannot transform what you cannot see. The writing is the seeing.

Second, practise the pause. The next time you feel a strong pull toward someone, or a strong dismissal of someone, stop. Place your hand on your chest and ask one question. Is this familiarity, or is this peace? Do not answer it yourself. Bring it to God and let Him answer. The honest answer may take days, or it may come in the moment. Either way, the asking is the practice, because the asking trains your mind to pause where it used to obey. Over time you will notice that what used to feel like nothing is starting to feel like peace, and what used to feel like everything is starting to feel like a cycle you no longer wish to play out. That is the renewing, working.

A prayer for this episode

God, show me where my wounds are showing up in my relationships.

Show me where I am testing instead of trusting. Chasing chaos instead of resting in calm. Giving from fear instead of from overflow.

I confess that I have walked past the steady ones because my wounds did not recognise them, and run toward the unstable ones because my wounds knew them by name. I confess that I have followed my heart in places where my heart was not yet a trustworthy witness.

Renew my mind. Heal the patterns, not just the surface behaviours but the root beliefs underneath them. Train my eyes to recognise peace. Train my body to rest in steadiness.

And teach me to stay with myself, so I can stop needing other people to stay for me in order to feel safe.

Amen.
