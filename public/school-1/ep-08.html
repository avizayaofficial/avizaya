School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 8

What real healing actually looks like

Healing is not a destination you arrive at. It is a direction you keep choosing. Every small choice in the right direction is the healing.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

There is a version of healing that looks like healing but is not. It is healing as performance. It is the woman who has done all the work, read all the books, knows all the language, posts all the right things about boundaries and self-worth. But inside nothing has actually shifted. Because the work stayed in the mind and never reached the body.

Real healing is embodied. It lives in the nervous system. It shows up in the choices you make when you are tired and scared and no one is watching. It shows up in whether you can stay in a calm, consistent relationship without sabotaging it, in whether you can say no without a flood of guilt, in whether you can sit in a quiet room with yourself and not immediately reach for something to fill the silence.

Part 1

What real healing looks like

Real healing is ugly. It is sitting with the pain you have been numbing for years and feeling it for the first time without reaching for the thing that makes it stop. It is having the conversation you have been avoiding. It is saying no for the first time and watching someone be disappointed with you and choosing to survive it.

It is choosing yourself in a small way on a Tuesday when no one is watching and no one is clapping and no one will ever know you did it. And then doing it again the next Tuesday. And the one after that. Until the choice to honour yourself becomes the default instead of the exception.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV

The Greek word for transformed is metamorphoo, the same word we get metamorphosis from. A caterpillar does not transform without first going into a dark, enclosed, uncomfortable place. It does not emerge looking the same, and it cannot return to what it was.

That is not a metaphor for a process that is comfortable and curated. That is a description of what actual change costs.

Part 2

The neuroplasticity of healing

Your brain is not fixed. Every time you make a different choice than the wound would have made for you, you are physically building new neural pathways. Every time you stay in a moment of discomfort instead of numbing it, you are teaching your nervous system that it can survive what it feared it could not.

This is not metaphor. This is neuroscience. And it is also Romans 12:2. The mind can be renewed and the patterns can be changed, but they are changed by repetition, not by insight.

Understanding that you have abandonment wounds is not healing. It is the beginning of healing. The healing is in the ten thousand small choices that follow the understanding.

Pain is not bad

I learned that pain is not bad. It is when you stop feeling the pain that it truly gets bad. Numbness is the real danger, not the pain. The pain is the signal, the signal that something inside you is still alive and asking to be tended to.

Part 3

The kintsugi principle in Scripture

Kintsugi is the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with gold. The philosophy is that the break is not something to hide. It is something to honour. The repaired vessel is more beautiful because of what it survived.

This is the theology of the cross. God does not erase scars. He redeems them. Jesus rose with the wounds still in His hands, and Thomas touched them. The wounds became the proof of the resurrection, not the shame of the crucifixion. What was meant to destroy became the evidence of what could not be destroyed.

"I will repay you for the years the locusts have eaten."

Joel 2:25 NIV

Not some of the years. The years. Every season that was stolen from you, every year you spent in the wrong place with the wrong people doing the wrong version of yourself, God says He can restore it. That is not wishful thinking. That is a covenant promise to a people who had lost everything. And it is a covenant promise to you.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What does healing look like in your life right now? Is it in your mind only or is it reaching your body and your choices?

Two. What is the hardest choice you keep avoiding that would mark real movement in your healing?

Three. What would it mean to let your scars become gold instead of something you hide?

Four. Which years are you still grieving? Can you bring them to God and trust the Joel 2:25 promise?

Five. What is one small thing you could do today that the wound would tell you not to do?

A prayer for this episode

God, I want real healing. Not performed healing. Not language without transformation.

Go into the places in me that are still numb. Bring feeling back, even if the feeling is pain, because at least pain means something is alive.

Renew my mind the way Romans 12 promises. Not just my understanding. My actual patterns. My actual choices. My actual nervous system.

Restore the years. I am believing You for every single one.

Amen.
