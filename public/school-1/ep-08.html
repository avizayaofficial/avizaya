School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 8

What real healing actually looks like

Healing is not a destination you arrive at. It is a direction you keep choosing. Every small choice in the right direction is the healing.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

There is a version of healing that looks like healing but is not. It is healing as performance. It is the woman who has done all the work, read all the books, knows all the language, posts all the right things about boundaries and self-worth. But inside nothing has actually shifted, because the work stayed in the mind and never reached the body.

There is a reason so many of us have been chasing that version. The culture has been selling a definition of healing that does not exist. It says a healed woman is one who is no longer affected, no longer triggered, no longer remembering, no longer feeling. That is not healing. That is numbness with a better name.

The Word of God names healing differently. The woman God heals is not a woman who has forgotten what happened to her. She is a woman who has been made whole around what happened to her, and the wholeness is the very thing that makes her formidable.

"He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds."

Psalm 147:3 NIV

He heals and He binds, and the binding is not the erasure of the wound. It is the careful gathering of the broken edges back together so they can knit into something stronger than they were before the breaking. The place where you were bound up becomes a place where God has been particularly close, and where you have become particularly aware of Him, because that is the place you most needed Him to come.

Real healing is embodied. It lives in the nervous system. It shows up in the choices you make when you are tired and scared and no one is watching, in whether you can stay in a calm, consistent relationship without sabotaging it, in whether you can say no without a flood of guilt, in whether you can sit in a quiet room with yourself and not immediately reach for something to fill the silence.

Part 1

What healing is not

Before we name what healing is, we have to clear away what it is not, because chasing a healing that does not exist is exhausting, and you have been tired long enough.

Real healing is not the absence of memory. You will remember what happened to you for the rest of your life. The memory is not the problem. The memory is a problem only while it is connected to a wound that has not been bound. Once the wound is bound, the memory remains but the charge is gone. You can think about what happened without your body bracing for impact. You can speak of it without your voice catching. The memory has become information instead of injury.

The soul heals the way the body heals. When skin is broken, a scar forms, and the scar remains. You can run your fingers over a scar from a childhood injury decades later and still feel where the skin had to knit back together. But the scar does not hurt anymore. The scar is the evidence that healing happened, not that the injury is still active. The same holds in the soul. The mark of what you survived stays visible, and that is not a failure of healing. It is what healing produces. You can touch the place and see where it happened, and the touching no longer brings pain.

The danger is not the scar. The danger is what we do to the wound before the scar can form. A wound that is rushed, ignored or repeatedly interrupted gets infected, and what could have been a quiet scar becomes a problem that spreads through the whole body. The soul works the same way. The woman who covers the wound without cleaning it, who declares herself healed before God has actually done the work, develops a kind of spiritual infection that shows up in places that seem unrelated to the original injury. And the woman who keeps picking at the scab is the one whose wound never finishes closing. Every rehearsal of the offence, every return to the conversation she should have left behind, every re-entry into the relationship that caused the injury pulls the healing open just before it could complete. God has been faithfully knitting the edges together, and she has been pulling them apart, often without realising it. Real healing asks you to leave the wound alone long enough for Him to finish what He started.

Real healing is not the absence of triggers either. Situations, people, songs and seasons will keep reminding you of what hurt you, sometimes for life. But the healing woman learns to greet the trigger with curiosity instead of fear. The rising emotion is information about what is still tender, and she knows where to bring the tenderness. She brings it to God, lets it rise, lets Him meet her in it, and lets the meeting do the deeper work that avoiding triggers never could.

And real healing is not the absence of feeling. Here is something I had to learn the hard way. Pain is not bad. It is when you stop feeling the pain that it truly gets bad. Numbness is the real danger, not the pain. The pain is the signal that something inside you is still alive and asking to be tended to. The woman who has felt nothing for years because she buried everything is not healed. The goal is the capacity to feel deeply without being destroyed by what you are feeling. The healed woman cries when something is sad, gets angry when something is unjust, grieves when something is lost and laughs from her belly when something is funny. Her emotional life is more vivid than it was when she was wounded, not less, because she is no longer spending her whole nervous system managing what is unfelt inside her.

Part 2

What real healing looks like

Real healing is ugly before it is beautiful. It is sitting with the pain you have been numbing for years and feeling it for the first time without reaching for the thing that makes it stop. It is having the conversation you have been avoiding. It is saying no for the first time and watching someone be disappointed with you and choosing to survive it.

It is choosing yourself in a small way on a Tuesday when no one is watching and no one is clapping and no one will ever know you did it. And then doing it again the next Tuesday, and the one after that, until the choice to honour yourself becomes the default instead of the exception.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV

The Greek word for transformed is metamorphoo, the same word we get metamorphosis from. A caterpillar does not transform without first going into a dark, enclosed, uncomfortable place. It does not emerge looking the same, and it cannot return to what it was. That is not a metaphor for a process that is comfortable and curated. That is a description of what actual change costs.

And your brain is built for exactly this. Every time you make a different choice than the wound would have made for you, you are physically building new neural pathways. Every time you stay in a moment of discomfort instead of numbing it, you are teaching your nervous system that it can survive what it feared it could not. This is neuroscience, and it is also Romans 12:2. The mind can be renewed and the patterns can be changed, but they are changed by repetition, not by insight. Understanding that you have abandonment wounds is not healing. It is the beginning of healing. The healing is in the ten thousand small choices that follow the understanding.

What those choices build, over time, is wholeness. The healing words of Scripture are woven from the same root as the words for wholeness and completeness, and in many New Testament passages the Greek word translated healed is the same word translated saved. That doubling is intentional. In God's design, the healing of a woman is part of the same work as the saving of her. The wounded woman is fragmented, with parts of her stuck in old seasons and patterns running that she cannot trace to a source. The healing woman is being gathered, brought home to herself, re-integrated one tender place at a time into the woman God formed before any wounding ever touched her. It changes the way she walks into a room. She is not divided against herself anymore, and everyone near her can feel it before they have words for it.

Part 3

You are not waiting for healing

"But he was pierced for our transgressions, he was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was on him, and by his wounds we are healed."

Isaiah 53:5 NIV

The wholeness was bought on the Cross, and we cannot move past that casually. The wounds you have been carrying have already been carried by Him. The price for your wholeness was paid before you knew you needed it paid.

This changes the entire orientation of your healing work. You are not earning your wholeness. You are not striving for it, not finally becoming worthy of it by working hard enough at your own recovery. The work has already been done. The wholeness is already yours. Your part is reception, not achievement, and the difference is the relief your striving has been refusing to receive. You have been waiting for permission to be whole, waiting to have earned it. God settled the question on the Cross. What remains is for you to walk into what has already been bought.

But walking in requires something from you, and there is a moment in the gospels that shows exactly what.

"When Jesus saw him lying there and learned that he had been in this condition for a long time, he asked him, 'Do you want to get well?'"

John 5:6 NIV

A man had been lying by the pool of Bethesda for thirty-eight years, waiting for someone to put him into the water when it stirred. Thirty-eight years of an identity built around being the one who could not get well. And when Jesus came to him, He did not heal him without asking. He asked first. "Do you want to get well?"

The question sounds strange until you sit with it. Jesus was honouring the man's will, because healing always involves a yes from the one being healed. A woman who has built her identity around her wound, who has organized her life around it and come to recognize herself by it, has to consent to becoming someone who is no longer defined by what hurt her. Without that consent the healing cannot reach all the way in, because part of her is still holding the wound as her sense of self.

The honest answer is harder than we expect. When you have been the wounded one for a long time, becoming the whole one can feel like a kind of grief, a small death of the self you have known. But the death is the doorway. Your life transforms the moment you stop living like the version of yourself that pain created and start living like the woman God intended. The wounded version of you is real. She has been real for years. But she is not the truest you. The truest you is the one God spoke into being before the wounding ever happened, and the healing is the work of letting her come out.

Part 4

The scars become gold and the years come back

Kintsugi is the Japanese art of repairing broken pottery with gold. The philosophy is that the break is not something to hide. It is something to honour. The repaired vessel is more beautiful because of what it survived.

This is the theology of the cross. God does not erase scars. He redeems them. Jesus rose with the wounds still in His hands, and Thomas touched them. The wounds became the proof of the resurrection, not the shame of the crucifixion. What was meant to destroy became the evidence of what could not be destroyed.

"I will repay you for the years the locusts have eaten."

Joel 2:25 NIV

Not some of the years. The years. The locusts are whatever has been consuming your seasons: the trauma, the wrong relationships, the numbing, the years you spent surviving when you should have been thriving. God names those years and promises restoration, which is more than forgetting and more than recovery. And the restoration follows a pattern you can already see in other women's lives. The woman who lost years to a wrong relationship ministers, a decade later, to women in wrong relationships with a depth no untouched woman could offer. The woman who lost years to the dark becomes the voice that pulls other women out of theirs, because she has been to the bottom and come back, and her testimony is the rope another woman grabs. The years are not lost. They come back transformed, holier than unmarked years could have been, because the years He gives back have His fingerprints on them.

"I will restore you to health and heal your wounds, declares the Lord."

Jeremiah 30:17 NIV

When God says I will, the thing is already done in eternity and only the unfolding remains in time. Your healing is not a hope. It is a promise. But God's promises never come without God's processes. He is not a vending machine. He is a vinedresser, and every day of the process is part of the fabric of the restoration itself. You are not delayed. You are being made.

"For our light and momentary troubles are achieving for us an eternal glory that far outweighs them all."

2 Corinthians 4:17 NIV

I did not experience healing the way I expected to. I expected a moment, a breakthrough, a morning when everything that had been hurting me would be lifted and I would emerge a new woman. What happened was slower and deeper and more beautiful than that. God did not erase what hurt me. He bound it, over years, so faithfully that one day I looked up and realised I was no longer the woman who had walked through the previous decade. The places that had once been my greatest pain had become the places I was most useful to the women He was sending into my life. I wrote these lines in the season when I understood that the breaking was not the end of me but the doorway:

And there I was, broken. And it was so beautiful, for now I was open to grow my heart and soul toward something greater still than they had ever known. But they couldn't until there was no more pride shown. But when I was broken, humility arose. My being unspoken, to next level transpose.

Part 5

The trap of service

There is one pattern that masquerades as healing so convincingly that we have to name it before this episode closes, because it keeps more women from real wholeness than almost any other dynamic.

The trap is service. The wounded woman often discovers, somewhere in her teens or twenties, that serving other people brings relief from her own pain. She helps a friend through a crisis and feels better for a day. She becomes the person everyone calls in their hardest moments, and slowly she becomes attached to the relief that helping gives her, because in the helping she gets to leave her own inner room for a while and live in someone else's.

The service looks holy. The church praises her for it, the family relies on it and the world calls her selfless. And underneath all of it she is hiding, because the giving has become the very thing keeping her from receiving, and she cannot heal what she is too busy avoiding to feel. Service is not the same as healing. It can become the most sophisticated form of self-abandonment a woman ever practices, precisely because it has been culturally sanctified.

Real healing requires you to be receiving from God before you can sustainably give from Him. The woman who pours out of an empty vessel eventually collapses, often in mid-life, confused about why her years of devotion have left her depleted instead of fulfilled. The order God established is the opposite. He pours into you first, and you pour out of the overflow. Allowing yourself to be ministered to before you minister, to be loved before you love, to be filled before you pour, is not selfishness. It is the prerequisite for everything else He is asking you to give.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What does healing look like in your life right now? Is it in your mind only, or is it reaching your body and your choices?

Two. Have you given God your yes to becoming a woman who is no longer defined by what hurt you? If not, which part of you is still holding the wound as your identity?

Three. What is the hardest choice you keep avoiding that would mark real movement in your healing?

Four. Which years are you still grieving as your locust years, and where can you already see God beginning to repay them in ways you had not noticed until now?

Five. What service have you been doing that has been masquerading as healing, and what would change if you let yourself receive before you give for one full week?

Three movements for this week

The first movement is to answer the hidden question. Sit quietly with God this week, alone, and let Him ask you what He asked the man at the pool. Do you want to get well? Answer honestly, and pay attention to the parts of you that hesitate, that have grown attached to the identity of the wounded one. Do not shame those parts. Bring them to Him. The yes does not have to be perfect to count. A trembling yes is still a yes, and a small honest yes is more powerful than a loud performed one.

The second movement is to receive before you give. For one week, before you do any of your usual serving, holding or fixing, sit with God for ten minutes and ask Him to fill you. Receive whatever He brings in those ten minutes. A scripture, a word, a peace, a tear. Only after you have been filled, step into the giving day that is waiting for you. This reverses the order that has been quietly depleting you for years. Practise it for one week and notice what has happened to your energy by Friday.

The third movement is to name one locust year that is being restored. Look back across your life and identify one specific year or season you would have called wasted. Now look at what God has been building in you since. What came out of it. Who you became because of it. What you can now offer other women that you could not have offered without walking through it. Write down the line from the lost year to the restored fruit. Naming it is part of the receiving, and the receiving is what makes the restoration real in your life instead of a doctrine you nod at on Sunday.

A prayer for this episode

God, I want real healing. Not performed healing. Not language without transformation.

I confess that I have been striving for what You already purchased for me. I confess that I have been serving from an empty vessel and calling it holiness. I confess that I have built parts of my identity around what hurt me, and that some of me is still afraid to let those parts go.

Go into the places in me that are still numb. Bring feeling back, even if the feeling is pain, because pain means something is still alive.

Renew my mind the way Romans 12 promises. Not just my understanding. My actual patterns, my actual choices, my actual nervous system.

I say yes to being a woman who is no longer defined by her wound. And I am believing You to restore the years, every single one.

Amen.
