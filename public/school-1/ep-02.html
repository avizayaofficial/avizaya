School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 2

You are not looking for an identity.

You are not looking for an identity. You are uncovering one. It was placed inside you before you took your first breath.

"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart."

Jeremiah 1:5 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

I want to ask you something before we go any further. And I want you to be honest, not with me, but with yourself.

Do you know who you are? Not your name, not your roles, not what you do for a living or what you look like when you are holding it together. I mean the real you. The one underneath the competence, underneath the giving, underneath the version of yourself you show to the world.

Because here is what I have learned: the women who are most lost are rarely the ones who look lost. They are the ones who look like they have it all together. The capable ones, the strong ones, the ones who always show up for everyone else, the ones who would never let you see them cry.

I was that woman for years. Terrifyingly capable on the outside, completely unraveling on the inside. And nobody knew, because I had learned very early that being seen in your pain was dangerous, that needing things was a burden, that the safest version of me was the version that needed nothing from anyone.

Part 1

When you do not know who you are

Satan's oldest strategy is not to destroy you. It is to make you forget who you are. Because a woman who does not know who she is will accept any identity that is handed to her. She will become whoever the room needs her to be. She will shrink or expand based on whoever is watching. She will build her entire sense of self on things that can be taken away: her appearance, her relationships, her achievements, her usefulness to others.

And then one day something is taken away, and she collapses. Not because she was weak. But because she built on sand.

"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart."

Jeremiah 1:5 NIV

God knew you before you had a name, before you had a face, before you had a history, before you had achievements or failures or relationships. He knew you. And He set you apart.

Your identity is not something you build. It is something you discover. It was placed inside you before you took your first breath.

Part 2

The five signs your identity is built on sand

The first sign: you change who you are depending on who is in the room. With your mother you become small, with your boss you become professional, with your friends you become fun. With your man you become whoever he seems to need. You are a master of adjustment. But you are exhausted, because you are not actually living. You are performing. And performing is not the same as existing.

The second sign: you can list what you do but you cannot tell me who you are. Your identity is your resume, your roles, your accomplishments. Strip those away and there is a panic underneath, because you have never learned who you are when you are not being useful to someone.

"For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do."

Ephesians 2:10 NIV

The Greek word is poiema, the word we get poem from. You are not God's employee. You are God's poem. He did not create you to perform. He created you to be. And before you can do the work, you have to know the poet.

The third sign: other people's opinions feel like facts. If someone is disappointed in you, you believe you have failed. If someone praises you, you feel temporarily real. You have handed other people the keys to your self-worth. And they do not know they are holding them.

The fourth sign: you feel most alive when you are giving, and most empty when you are alone, because you have never developed a relationship with yourself. Solitude does not feel like rest. It feels like abandonment. You cannot stand to be alone with the person you have not yet learned to know.

The fifth sign: you do not know what you want. Not really. You know what other people need from you and you know what is expected of you. But if someone sat across from you and said, what do you actually want, your body would not know how to answer. Because for so long you have been so busy making sure everyone else was taken care of that you stopped listening to yourself entirely.

Part 3

Eve and the oldest lie

Genesis 3. The serpent comes to Eve and asks her one question. Did God really say? And then he offers her something. You will be like God, knowing good and evil.

Here is what wrecked me when I finally understood it. Eve was already made in the image of God. She already had within her everything the serpent was promising. The temptation only worked because she did not know who she already was.

Satan did not destroy Eve with something foreign. He destroyed her with something she already had, by convincing her she didn't have it. The temptation was not about the fruit. It was about identity.

Satan is still using the same tactic on you today. Every time you chase someone else's approval to feel valuable, you are Eve in the garden. Every time you shrink yourself to make someone else comfortable, you are Eve in the garden. Every time you let someone else's opinion of you become your fact, you are Eve in the garden. Not because you are weak. But because you do not yet know what you already carry.

"So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them."

Genesis 1:27 NIV
Part 4

How to find what was always there

Identity is not built. It is uncovered. The work is not to create a new self. The work is to strip away everything that was placed on top of the self you were always meant to be.

I spent years trying to become someone worthy of love. I dieted, achieved, performed, gave, served, smiled. And none of it filled the thing that was empty, because the thing that was empty was not a hole in my accomplishments. It was a hole in my knowledge of who I was before the world got to me.

The healing began when I stopped asking, what do I need to become, and started asking, who did God say I already am?

The practice of identity

Every day this week, before you perform anything for anyone, sit with this question: Who am I when no one needs anything from me?

Not what you do, not what you provide, not how you show up. Who are you, in the silence, before the world asks anything of you? That is where your real identity lives.

Questions to sit with this week

One. When did you first learn to perform instead of exist? What was the situation that taught you your true self was not enough?

Two. Whose voice is loudest in your head when you think about who you are? Is it God's voice? Or someone else's?

Three. If you stripped away every role you carry right now, who would be left? Can you sit with that person without panic?

Four. Where in your life are you still being Eve in the garden? Still chasing something you already possess but haven't recognised?

Five. What would change tomorrow if you actually believed you were made in the image of God?

A prayer for this episode

God, I have spent so long performing that I have forgotten how to just be.

Show me who I am underneath all of it. Not who the world needs me to be. Not who I have tried to become. Who You made me before anyone else had a word to say.

I renounce the lie that I have to earn my place. I renounce the lie that my value is in my usefulness. I renounce every identity that was handed to me by someone who was not You.

Teach me what it means to be made in Your image. Let that knowledge go deep enough that no one can ever take it from me again.

Amen.
