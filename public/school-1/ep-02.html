School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 2

You are not looking for an identity.

You are not looking for an identity. You are uncovering one, and it was placed inside you before you took your first breath.

"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart."

Jeremiah 1:5 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

I want to ask you something before we go any further, and I want you to be honest with yourself.

Do you know who you are? Not your name, not your roles, not what you do for a living or what you look like when you are holding it together. I mean the real you. The one underneath the competence, underneath the giving, underneath the version of yourself you show to the world. The one who exists when the door has closed behind you, the phone has gone dark and no one is asking anything of you anymore. She is the only you that is truly real, because she is the only one who is not being shaped by whoever is watching.

What I have learned is that the women who are most lost are rarely the ones who look lost. They are the ones who look like they have it all together. The capable ones, the strong ones, the ones who always show up for everyone else, the ones who would never let you see them cry.

I was that woman for years. Terrifyingly capable on the outside, completely unraveling on the inside. And nobody knew, because I had learned very early that being seen in your pain was dangerous, that needing things was a burden, that the safest version of me was the version that needed nothing from anyone.

In our last conversation we laid the first stone of this school, Psalm 27:10, the promise that though our father and mother forsake us, the Lord Himself receives us. This second teaching sits directly on top of that one. Once you know who is holding you, the very next question is who you are inside His hands.

"For you created my inmost being; you knit me together in my mother's womb. I praise you because I am fearfully and wonderfully made; your works are wonderful, I know that full well."

Psalm 139:13-14 NIV

Before your father held you, before your mother gave you a name, before culture told you who to be and how to look and what to want from your one short life, before the wounds and the words and the labels had ever found their way to you, the woman God knit together in your mother's womb was already there. Our work in this episode is not to invent her. Our work is to remember her.

Part 1

When you do not know who you are

Satan's oldest strategy is not to destroy you. It is to make you forget who you are. Because a woman who does not know who she is will accept any identity that is handed to her. She will become whoever the room needs her to be. She will shrink or expand based on whoever is watching. She will build her entire sense of self on things that can be taken away, her appearance, her relationships, her achievements, her usefulness to others.

And then one day something is taken away, and she collapses. Not because she was weak, but because she built on sand.

"Before I formed you in the womb I knew you, before you were born I set you apart."

Jeremiah 1:5 NIV

Before. Before you were anything that anyone in this world could see, you were known by the only One whose eyes were the first to see you. God knew you before you had a name, before you had a face, before you had a history, before you had achievements or failures or relationships. He knew you, and He set you apart. That identity was established before you ever arrived in the world that would later try to redefine you, and it has never once been revoked.

So many of us walked through our whole lives without ever being told that we were known first by God, and so we let everyone else do the telling for Him. Our parents told us who we were, and we believed them. Our culture told us, and we adapted ourselves to fit. The boys who liked us told us, and the boys who did not told us, and the friends who left told us by their leaving. The teachers who overlooked us told us by their overlooking. And we mistook all of that for who we actually were.

But none of that is who you are. All of that is who you were assigned to be by people who did not form you, and not one of them had the authority to define you in the first place. Your identity is not something you build. It is something you uncover, because it was placed inside you before you took your first breath.

Part 2

The five signs your identity is built on sand

The first sign is that you change who you are depending on who is in the room. With your mother you become small, with your boss you become professional, with your friends you become fun, with your man you become whoever he seems to need. You are a master of adjustment. But you are exhausted, because you are not actually living. You are performing, and performing is not the same as existing.

The second sign is that you can list what you do but you cannot tell me who you are. Your identity is your resume, your roles, your accomplishments. Strip those away and there is a panic underneath, because you have never learned who you are when you are not being useful to someone.

"For we are God's handiwork, created in Christ Jesus to do good works, which God prepared in advance for us to do."

Ephesians 2:10 NIV

The Greek word behind handiwork is poiema, the word we get poem from. You are not God's employee. You are God's poem. He did not create you to perform. He created you to be. But before you can do the work, you have to know the Poet.

The third sign is that other people's opinions feel like facts. If someone is disappointed in you, you believe you have failed. If someone praises you, you feel temporarily real. You have handed other people the keys to your self-worth, when your self-worth was determined by God alone.

The fourth sign is that you feel most alive when you are giving and most empty when you are alone, because you have never developed a relationship with yourself. Solitude does not feel like rest. It feels like abandonment. You cannot stand to be alone with the person you have not yet learned to know.

The fifth sign is that you do not really know what you want. You know what other people need from you and you know what is expected of you. But if someone sat across from you and asked what you actually want, your body would not know how to answer, because for so long you have been so busy making sure everyone else was taken care of that you stopped listening to yourself entirely.

Part 3

Eve and the oldest lie

Genesis 3. The serpent comes to Eve and asks her one question. Did God really say? And then he offers her something.

"'You will not certainly die,' the serpent said to the woman. 'For God knows that when you eat from it your eyes will be opened, and you will be like God, knowing good and evil.'"

Genesis 3:4-5 NIV

Hear what the serpent actually whispered to her. You will be like God. He was telling Eve, in the gentlest voice he could find, that she was missing something. He was suggesting that the woman God had just formed with His own hands was somehow incomplete, that there was a higher version of her she did not yet have access to.

This is what wrecked me when I finally understood it. Eve was already made in the image of God! She already had within her everything the serpent was promising. The temptation only worked because in that moment she lost contact with who she already was.

Satan did not destroy Eve with something foreign. He destroyed her with something she already had, by convincing her she did not have it. The temptation was not about the fruit. It was about identity. And the whole strategy depended entirely on her forgetting what God had said about her in the beginning, because a woman who knows she already bears the image of God cannot be persuaded to reach for what she has already been given. The enemy can only steal what you have forgotten you possess.

"So God created mankind in his own image, in the image of God he created them; male and female he created them."

Genesis 1:27 NIV

He is still using the same tactic today. Every time you chase someone else's approval to feel valuable, you are Eve in the garden. Every time you shrink yourself to make someone else comfortable, you are Eve in the garden. Every time you let someone else's opinion of you become your fact, you are Eve in the garden. Not because you are weak. Because you do not yet know what you already carry.

And this same attack runs through the entire Word of God. When Christ stood in the wilderness, the enemy did not begin by tempting Him with bread or with power. He began by attacking His identity.

"If you are the Son of God, tell these stones to become bread."

Matthew 4:3 NIV

If. The very first word out of his mouth. If you are who God said you are, then prove it. If the Father really loves you, then perform for me. But Christ did not take the bait. He did not need to prove anything to a voice He already knew was a liar from the beginning. He stood in His identity without performing it, and that quiet certainty defeated the temptation. The principle He modeled there is the one you are learning to walk in now. You do not have to prove who you are to the voices that question you. You only have to know who you are, and the knowing is its own protection.

Part 4

The layers you have been wearing

So here is the question: Whose voice has been telling you who you are?

For so many of us, the honest answer is everyone except God. The mother who praised us when we performed well and went quiet when we did not, teaching us by her silences that love comes only when we earn it. The father who looked at us sideways and made us wonder what was wrong with us. The culture that decided what beauty was, what success was, what a good woman was supposed to want. The internet that whispers, in a thousand soft and persistent ways, that the woman you are is not the woman you should be becoming.

Each one of these voices laid something onto you to the point that the woman you walk around in today is not really you. She is you covered. So many veils have been placed on top of you over so many years that the original woman God formed has been buried beneath them, and sometimes buried so deeply that you cannot even remember her name.

There are layers you may never have noticed you were putting on. The good girl layer, put on the day you learned that being chosen depended on being agreeable. The strong woman layer, put on the day you learned that no one was coming to save you, so you had to become the one who does all the saving. The spiritual performer layer, the achiever layer, the caretaker layer, the peacemaker layer, the funny one, the silent one. Some of these layers protected you in their season, and you can be grateful for the protection they gave you when you had nothing else. But not one of them is who you are.

If you are the woman who has worn the mask of competence so long that you no longer know you are wearing it, hear me: The mask is not the enemy. The mask was a survival strategy, and it worked. It got you through what would have destroyed a less resilient woman. It is a testament to your strength that you built it. But the mask was always meant to be temporary, a covering until you got somewhere safe, and the place you have been trying to get to all this time is here, with God, in the truth of who you actually are. The mask is now the very thing standing between you and the rest you have been working your whole life to deserve.

So the work of this school is excavation, not invention. You do not need to become a new woman. You need to remember the one God already made, because she is in there, and every layer that did not come from Him needs to come off. This is not psychological self-discovery. This is spiritual restoration. The world tells you to look inside yourself for the answers, because the world has forgotten the One who put the answers into you to begin with. We are going to look to the One who made you, because He alone holds the original blueprint.

Part 5

What He sees

"But the Lord said to Samuel, 'Do not consider his appearance or his height, for I have rejected him. The Lord does not look at the things people look at. People look at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart.'"

1 Samuel 16:7 NIV

Let me tell you the story behind that verse. The prophet Samuel had been sent to anoint the next king of Israel, and Jesse paraded his impressive sons in front of him, one after another. The eldest was tall and striking, and Samuel was so certain this must be the one that he was already preparing the oil. God said no. Seven sons passed before the prophet, each one looking the part, and God said no to every single one.

Then they had to call David in from the fields, because no one in his own family had even thought to invite him to the room where his own future was being decided. He had been overlooked his entire life by people who could not see past the surface. But God had been watching him in those fields for years, and God knew exactly who David was underneath all the overlooking.

That is the principle. People see the surface, and they cannot help it. God sees underneath, because seeing underneath is the very nature of His love. The room can be fooled. God cannot be. You do not have to convince Him, because He already knows you. You do not have to prove yourself to Him, because He already chose you. He prefers your honesty to your polish, your raw heart to your composed performance. The God who looks at the heart is the only audience whose opinion is permanent, and He settled His opinion about you long before the world ever had the chance to audition you.

So the work of returning is straightforward, though not fast. We strip the layers one at a time. Every time a belief surfaces, or a self-judgment, or an old fear, we ask one quiet question. Where did this come from? Not from God, then from whom? From your mother, from a man who left and took a piece of your identity with him, from a teacher who said something cruel when you were eight, from a culture that defined beauty in a way that excluded you. Whatever it is, we name it, we hold it up to the light of who God formed in the beginning, and if it does not match, we take it off, gently, with thanks for what it once was, but without keeping it a day longer than it needs to stay.

Scripture has a beautiful word for this work. It is called pruning.

"I am the true vine, and my Father is the gardener. He cuts off every branch in me that bears no fruit, while every branch that does bear fruit he prunes so that it will be even more fruitful."

John 15:1-2 NIV

Pruning is not destruction. Pruning is liberation. The gardener removes the branches that have been draining the vine without producing fruit, not because the vine has done something wrong, but because he wants more fruit than the vine could ever produce while carrying branches it was never meant to carry. Every borrowed layer of identity is a branch your Father is asking to prune, and He is not unkind for removing it now. What He is positioning you for cannot grow underneath a tangle of branches that were never yours.

The woman who lets Him do this work becomes unshakeable. The world cannot define her, because God already did. The room cannot move her, because she is rooted in the only opinion that ultimately matters. She walks differently, she speaks differently and she loves differently, because she has finally come to know who she actually is.

Questions to sit with this week

Read one a day. Sit with it, and bring it to God in prayer.

One. When did you first learn to perform instead of exist? What was the situation that taught you your true self was not enough?

Two. Whose voice is loudest in your head when you think about who you are? Is it God's voice, or someone else's, and where did that voice come from in the first place?

Three. If you stripped away every role you carry right now, who would be left? Can you sit with that person without panic?

Four. Where in your life are you still being Eve in the garden, chasing something you already possess but have not recognized?

Five. If you truly believed today what God says about you in Psalm 139 and Jeremiah 1:5, what is the first thing you would stop doing, and what would you finally let yourself begin?

The practice of identity

Every day this week, before you perform anything for anyone, sit with this question. Who am I when no one needs anything from me? Not what you do, not what you provide, not how you show up. Who are you in the silence, before the world asks anything of you? That is where your real identity lives.

Then carry three movements with you through the week.

The first movement is an audit. Every time you catch yourself making a decision, pause and ask whose voice is in my head right now, whose definition am I trying to satisfy, whose approval am I performing for. Hannah was barren, and her culture told her this made her less of a woman. She carried that voice for years, until the day she carried the whole burden into the presence of God and stopped carrying what was never hers. Write down the beliefs about yourself you have been quietly operating by, then go through them slowly and ask of each one, did God say this about me, or did someone else? The beliefs that did not come from God do not get to stay.

The second movement is a return to what God said. You cannot replace a borrowed identity with nothing. You replace it with truth. Find three scriptures about who you are in God and speak them out loud over yourself every morning, before the world has a chance to speak its old definitions first. Mary called herself the Lord's servant before she was anyone's mother or anyone's wife. Her identity was anchored in God first, and every other relationship flowed rightly from that anchor. What we speak rewrites what we have been hearing.

The third movement is one private act of alignment. Identity becomes real not through public declaration but through small private acts no one will ever see except you and God. Esther stepped into her royal identity not by announcing it but by quietly living it, and the living of it changed a nation. So do one thing this week that the woman God formed would do, something you have been avoiding because some old borrowed voice told you that you were not yet allowed to be her. Do it when no one is watching, because the only audience that ever mattered has been watching the whole time.

A prayer for this episode

God, I have spent so long performing that I have forgotten how to just be.

I confess that I have looked to people and to performance to tell me who I should become. I have worn masks I did not even know I was wearing. I have built a self out of what others expected of me, and the self I built has not held me.

Show me who I am underneath all of it. Not who the world needs me to be. Not who I have tried to become. Who You made me before anyone else had a word to say.

I renounce the lie that I have to earn my place. I renounce the lie that my value is in my usefulness. I renounce every identity that was handed to me by someone who was not You.

Teach me what it means to be made in Your image, and let that knowledge go deep enough that no one can ever take it from me again. Help me remember the woman You formed, and then help me become her, one small private act of alignment at a time.

Amen.

Who you are when no one is watching is the only you that was ever truly real, and everything else was always too small a destiny for the woman God formed.
