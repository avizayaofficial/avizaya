School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 4

Healing from mother abandonment.

Your mother did the best she could with what she had. And what she could not give, God has been holding for you all along. You are allowed to receive it now.

"Can a mother forget the baby at her breast and have no compassion on the child she has borne? Though she may forget, I will not forget you."

Isaiah 49:15 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

The mother wound is quieter than the father wound. It does not announce itself the same way. It just sits inside you as a persistent, wordless feeling that you were never quite enough for the one person who was supposed to love you without condition.

It is also the wound you have been most discouraged from naming. Culture allows a daughter to grieve a father who failed her. Culture rarely permits a daughter to grieve a mother who did the same. Mothers are sacred in every culture on earth, and rightly so, because the work of mothering is one of the holiest works a woman ever does. But the sacredness of the role does not make every woman who entered it capable of fulfilling it. So the daughter who carries this wound has often carried it alone, in silence, with the constant guilt of even noticing it.

Today we are going to name it. Not to dishonor anyone, and not to assign blame. To begin healing something that has been quietly running underneath your relationship with yourself, with other women, with your own body, with your own children and with God Himself, for longer than you have been willing to admit.

God chose the image in Isaiah 49 deliberately. A mother's love was supposed to be the most unbreakable love on earth, so He uses it as the ultimate standard of faithfulness. Notice that He does not deny that mothers sometimes forget. He names it as a real possibility, and then He uses that very possibility as the canvas for His own promise. Even if she forgets, I will not. The God of the universe has positioned Himself as the One who picks up exactly where a mother could not, and He spoke it into His Word so that no daughter would ever have to wonder whether her ache had been seen.

My mother loved me. She also hurt me in ways that took me years to name. Both things are true, and the healing required me to hold both without collapsing one to protect the other.

Part 1

What the mother wound actually is

Your mother was the first vessel of love you ever knew. Hers was the first heartbeat you heard, while you were still inside her body, and hers was the first face you searched for the moment you were placed outside of it. Before you had language, before you had memory, she was teaching you, through her presence or her absence, through her warmth or her coldness, what love was going to feel like for the rest of your life. If she was warm and responsive, you learned that love is reliable. If she was cold or anxious or unpredictable, you learned that love is something to chase, to manage, to never quite trust.

The mother wound is not always about a mother who left. Sometimes it is about a mother who stayed but was not present. Who was physically there but emotionally somewhere else. Who loved you the best she could but whose best was not enough for what you needed.

The daughter who did not receive sufficient mothering grows up with a hunger she cannot name, and she mistakes that hunger for many things across her life. She mistakes it for romantic longing and pours it into men. She mistakes it for ambition and pours it into work. She mistakes it for hospitality and pours it into mothering everyone around her, at the expense of ever being mothered herself. The hunger is real. The targets have not been able to satisfy it, because the original source of the hunger was a vessel that was empty when she first reached for it.

My mother raised ten children alone in a foreign country after losing my father and two siblings in one car accident. She was one of the strongest women I have ever known. She was also one of the most wounded, and the wound she carried she passed on to us, not because she wanted to, but because that is how unhealed wounds travel. Through the generations, through the body, through the things that are never said and the love that is never shown.

For most of my childhood I never heard the words I love you from her. Not once. I did not know until I was much older that she had never heard them from her own parents either. She could not give what she had never received.

That is the mother wound. It is not always malice. Often it is just the cost of a lineage of women who never learned how to be held.

"Can a mother forget the baby at her breast and have no compassion on the child she has borne? Though she may forget, I will not forget you."

Isaiah 49:15 NIV
Part 2

The faces of the wound

The mother wound comes in many shapes, and the shape matters, because the healing has to meet the specific shape of the wound and not a generic version of it.

Some mothers were physically absent, through death, through addiction, through their own broken inheritance, through a choice to build a life somewhere their daughter was not. The daughter grew up with a literal hole where a mother was supposed to be, and she has been quietly mothering herself ever since, doing what no child was designed to do for herself.

Some mothers were present but unavailable. They cooked the meals and washed the clothes and did the visible work of mothering, but they were not there with the daughter in the way she needed. That daughter had a mother who was always around and still felt, somehow, deeply alone in her own house.

Some mothers were so wounded that the daughter became the mother of the relationship, responsible for managing her mother's emotions, for being the strong one because her mother was too fragile to be. The role reversal happened so early that the daughter does not remember a time when she was not holding her own mother up.

Some mothers competed with their daughters, for attention, for the spotlight, for the title of the most beautiful woman in the room. That daughter learned her own emergence as a woman was a threat, and she shrank to protect a relationship she should have been free to grow inside. Some mothers controlled, and the love had conditions the daughter spent her life performing for. Some mothers criticized, and the daughter learned that love and criticism arrive together, and she has been confusing the two ever since.

Each of these shapes carries the same wound at the root. She did not mother me the way I needed, and so I have been moving through the world with a hunger no human relationship has been able to satisfy, and I have not understood why.

And here is how the wound shows up now. It shows up as a woman who does not know how to receive nurture, who feels guilty when she rests, who takes care of everyone and cannot ask to be taken care of in return. Who apologises for her needs and is tender with everyone except herself, because she never had a model of a woman who is also allowed to be held. It shows up as a woman who over-mothers others to fill the space inside herself that was never mothered, who gives endlessly to children, friends and strangers, and then one day wakes up empty and does not know why. It shows up as a woman who is fierce and capable and exhausted and who has never once sat down and asked herself: who is taking care of me?

Part 3

The God who mothers

The same God who named Himself Father to the fatherless has also taken on mother imagery for the daughter whose mother could not hold her. This is one of the most overlooked truths in the Word of God. He is most often called Father, and rightly so. But the same Word gives Him mother language, deliberately and tenderly, in the very places where mother love is what a wounded daughter most needs to receive.

"As a mother comforts her child, so will I comfort you; and you will be comforted over Jerusalem."

Isaiah 66:13 NIV

God compares His own comfort to the comfort of a mother. The deepest kind of comfort, the kind that does not solve the problem so much as it absorbs the pain, the kind that holds a daughter while she weeps and does not require her to stop weeping in order to be loved.

"How often I have longed to gather your children together, as a hen gathers her chicks under her wings, and you were not willing."

Matthew 23:37 NIV

This is Christ Himself, standing over Jerusalem, weeping. The image He reaches for is not a king summoning subjects or a general organizing an army. It is a mother hen, low to the ground, calling her babies in under the warmth of her own body to keep them safe.

"The Lord your God is with you, the Mighty Warrior who saves. He will take great delight in you; in his love he will no longer rebuke you, but will rejoice over you with singing."

Zephaniah 3:17 NIV

He rejoices over you with singing. That is a mother's love. The hum over the sleeping child, the warmth in the voice. God Himself does that over you.

You are not lacking the source. You may have lacked the vessel.

And He does not stop at His own presence. He sends other women to mother you, not to replace your mother but to restore what was missed, in pieces and in seasons. A grandmother who held you. An aunt who saw you. A teacher who believed in you when no one in your own house did. An older friend who taught you how a woman is supposed to be loved. This is the pattern across Scripture. Naomi mothered Ruth, Elizabeth mothered Mary, and the older women of Titus 2 are told to teach the younger, because God designed the family of faith as a place where mothering flows to the women who need it. Look back across your life and start to see them. Each of those women, in her time, was a piece of the mothering God was determined to give you.

Part of your healing also requires that you become to yourself what your mother could not be. Not in bitterness, not as a replacement, but in tenderness. The little girl inside you who needed to be held, who needed someone to say you are safe, she is still there. You are the adult now, and you are the one who can finally give her what she waited for.

I had to learn this the hard way. For years I took care of everyone around me with a devotion I would never have directed at myself, and then I realised I was giving to others what I had never allowed myself to receive. The healing was not in giving more. It was in finally learning how to be on the receiving end. Of rest, of tenderness, of the words: you have done enough, you are allowed to stop. If you became the mother you did not have, if you made a vow that your children would never feel what you felt, honor that work, because breaking a pattern with no model to copy from is holy work. But you cannot keep pouring from a cup no one is filling. The mothering you give will pour out of your own ache until you let yourself be mothered too. By God, and by the women He has positioned around you. The cup needs to be filled, and He has been waiting for you to bring it.

Part 4

Forgiving her and honoring her anyway

"Honor your father and your mother, so that you may live long in the land the Lord your God is giving you."

Exodus 20:12 NIV

The commandment is unconditional. It does not say honor your mother if she honored you first, or provided she earned it by the way she treated you. It simply says honor her, and it attaches a blessing to the daughter who does. Honoring a mother who wounded you is one of the most spiritually freeing acts a daughter ever does. Not because she deserves it. Because the act of honoring her, in spite of what she did or did not give, releases you from a bondage you have been carrying without knowing it.

This does not mean pretending, and it does not mean rewriting the story or putting yourself back into harm's way if she is still actively harmful. Boundaries and honor are not mutually exclusive. A daughter can honor a mother from a distance, with prayer, with a refusal to speak bitterness about her into the air, with a quiet refusal to repeat her patterns into the next generation.

But the heart shift has to happen, and the heart shift is forgiveness. Think about this. Forgive means for-give, to give away. The only thing still connecting you to the one who hurt you is the pain, and that pain did not begin in you. It lived in her first, and she shared it with you because that is what unhealed pain does. Forgiveness is how you give the pain away, back out of your hands, out of your body, out of your future. It is not a gift to her. It is a gift God gave to you, so that you could finally detach from what was never yours to carry. We walked through this fully in episode 3, and it applies to your mother just as completely as it applied to your father.

Forgiveness does not require her participation, her apology or even her awareness. It only requires your willingness, in the presence of God, to release what is no longer yours to hold. The mother who hurt you may never feel the weight of your unforgiveness, but you feel it every day, because bitterness corrodes the vessel that holds it long before it reaches the one it was aimed at. When you release it, you are walking in the pattern Christ walked in, and the walking of it sets you free in a way no journal and no inner work ever could on its own.

Part 5

Breaking the cycle

"I will pour water on the thirsty land, and streams on the dry ground; I will pour out my Spirit on your offspring, and my blessing on your descendants."

Isaiah 44:3 NIV

The healing you do today is not just for you. It is for every daughter and granddaughter who comes after you. When you heal the wound, you break the chain. That is what generational impact looks like. Not just building a legacy for your descendants to inherit. Removing the wounds they would have otherwise inherited instead.

I want to give you the evidence that this is possible, because I have lived it.

My mother was born in 1960 in a small village in Belarus, to parents who were severe alcoholics. She grew up in abuse and hard labor, and she never once heard I love you from her own parents. She survived more than many women survive in a lifetime, and when my father and two of my siblings died in that accident, she was left a widow in a country whose language she did not speak, and days before the funeral she learned she was pregnant. I cannot imagine the grief she carried. The pain she could not put down came out sideways onto her children, because a wound that has not been healed at the root will keep finding ways to express itself through the woman carrying it. What I heard growing up was not I love you. What I heard was that it would have been easier if she never had me, and that I was a punishment from God. When I was fourteen she remarried and moved away, and I stayed behind. The daughter who had been told she was a punishment now had what felt like the proof of it. That wound nearly took my life in the years that followed.

The year after high school, in ministry school, I understood that if I was ever going to love myself, the work began with one impossible task. I had to forgive my mother. I did not know how. So I made it my prayer for the year, and I made one wish I did not think was possible. God, please let me hear the words I love you from my mother at least once before I die.

I began calling her every month. For over six months, every call went the same way. She would preach at me for twenty minutes, criticize how I lived and how I dressed, and at the end of every call I would say I love you. And every single time her answer was the same. I don't need that kind of love. Then she would hang up, and I would cry. But I had decided I would not give up. I called more often, weekly instead of monthly. I kept working on me, blessing her and praying for us both, because I was no longer waiting on God to change my mother while God was waiting on me to do the work that was mine.

Then one day she stopped saying it. I told her I loved her and she simply hung up, and I cried for a different reason that day, because those tears were gratitude. Months later the conversations transformed. She started telling me about her day, the new calf on her little farm, the chickens she had bought. She began asking when I would visit. And on June 22, 2015, almost exactly a year after I started, she called me on my lunch break, and ten minutes into the conversation she said it. Dochinka, ya lublu tebya. Daughter, I love you. In Russian, the words I had never heard from her in my life. They came out of her like something released, like a long-held breath, and I could hear that they surprised her as much as they surprised me. God had done in her what I could not do. Today, I still tell her I love her every time we talk, even though she never says it back.

You cannot change the mother who hurt you. You can only change yourself, bless her and pray for you both, and then God does what only He can do, which is to soften a heart no human force could soften and put words into a mouth that did not know how to form them.

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here."

2 Corinthians 5:17 NIV

Whatever was handed down to you that did not come from God does not have to keep traveling through you. Your daughters will not inherit what you inherited. Some of the women in your family line waited generations for one daughter to finally do this work. You are that daughter. The cycle can be broken, and it starts with you.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What did your mother not give you that a mother is meant to give? Name it specifically, in the language you have been afraid to use even with yourself.

Two. What did your mother model to you about how a woman treats herself? Was it wholeness or depletion?

Three. Which women has God sent across your life to mother you in pieces and in seasons? Have you been receiving them, or holding them at the distance you learned to keep with your own mother?

Four. Have you forgiven your mother, not to excuse what she did but to free yourself from carrying it? Where is unforgiveness still sitting in you, and what might release set free?

Five. What cycle are you committed to ending with you, so it does not reach your daughters?

One small permission

This week, one practice. Give yourself, with no apology and no explanation to anyone, the one form of mothering you wish you had received and never did. Not in a grand way. In a small one.

Maybe it is rest, because no one ever told you that you were allowed to rest. Maybe it is a quiet morning with a cup of tea and a book that has nothing to do with productivity. Maybe it is preparing yourself a meal with the same care you would prepare it for someone you love. Maybe it is wrapping yourself in a blanket and letting yourself cry for ten minutes without anyone interrupting you and without trying to fix what is rising in you.

Whatever it is, give it to yourself this week, and recognize what you are doing as you do it. This is not indulgence. You are receiving what should have been freely given to you long ago and was not. The receiving is the work, and the work is healing you while you do it, even when you cannot feel the healing happening. God has been mothering you all along. The practice this week is to finally let Him.

A prayer for this episode

God, You are the mother love I never fully received from a human source.

Teach me to receive from You what was missing. Teach me to rest, to be held, to be nurtured without guilt.

I bring You my mother. The woman she was and the woman she could not be. I lay her at Your feet, not in judgment but in release, because I cannot keep carrying her in the place where I needed to be carried.

Help me mother the little girl inside me the way You mother me. With patience. With tenderness. With the hum of a song over a sleeping child.

Break the cycle in me, and let the healing I receive today pour forward into every generation that carries my name.

Amen.
