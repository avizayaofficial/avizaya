School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 6

Trauma bonding: why you keep returning to what hurts you.

The bond feels like love because pain and relief were always packaged together. But real love does not wound you to make the relief feel necessary.

"My people have committed two sins: They have forsaken me, the spring of living water, and have dug their own cisterns, broken cisterns that cannot hold water."

Jeremiah 2:13 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

You know it is wrong, you know it is hurting you, you have left a hundred times in your head. And still you go back. And every time you go back you hate yourself a little more, and the self-hatred makes the wound deeper, and the deeper wound makes it harder to leave.

This is trauma bonding. It is one of the most sophisticated traps the human nervous system can build, and until you understand it you will keep living inside it.

The world treats the trauma bond as a clinical term, something that only happens in extreme cases. The truth is that a woman who carries the wounds of the earlier episodes in this school has often been forming these bonds in some form for most of her life and calling them love. The Word of God has a name for this dynamic, and the name is bondage.

"It is for freedom that Christ has set us free. Stand firm, then, and do not let yourselves be burdened again by a yoke of slavery."

Galatians 5:1 NIV

Any relationship, any pattern, any attachment that masters you the way only God should master you is a yoke you were never meant to wear. And many of us have been wearing yokes for a very long time without recognizing them, because the yokes had names like love and loyalty and faithfulness, and the world taught us to romanticize what God has always called bondage.

Part 1

What trauma bonding is

A trauma bond forms when cycles of pain are interspersed with periods of warmth and closeness. The unpredictability creates a biochemical addiction. The relief after the pain feels like love, and the brain links the person with both the wound and the relief. So leaving feels like losing the one thing that can make the pain stop.

The hot and cold, the push and pull, the cruelty followed by the tenderness that makes you doubt what you saw. That is not love. That is a mechanism. And your nervous system has been trained to respond to it as though it were love, because that is what love looked like when you were learning what love was.

The technical name for the engine underneath this is intermittent reinforcement, and it is the same principle that keeps a person sitting at a slot machine. Unpredictable cycles of reward and withdrawal create a stronger attachment than consistent reward ever could. The woman who knows what to expect can walk away when what she gets is not enough. The woman who never quite knows what she will get, but who has tasted enough of the good to keep hoping it returns, becomes the woman who cannot walk away no matter what the bad is costing her. The unpredictability is the cage.

And the bond does not only appear in romance. It shows up in the friendship that drains you and then offers just enough kindness to keep you returning. It shows up with the parent who criticizes for years and then has the rare warm moment that keeps you hoping for a closeness that never quite arrives. It shows up with the boss who praises you publicly and undermines you privately. The dynamic is the same wherever it appears. The cycle of harm and relief, the loyalty to who they could be instead of who they are, the exhaustion of holding the relationship together by yourself while the other person rests. Whatever name the relationship wears, this teaching is for you.

"My people have committed two sins: They have forsaken me, the spring of living water, and have dug their own cisterns, broken cisterns that cannot hold water."

Jeremiah 2:13 NIV

The broken cistern cannot hold water. But you keep going back to it because you have not yet fully believed that the spring of living water is real, or available to you, or something you deserve.

Part 2

Why the abandonment wound makes you vulnerable

If you grew up in an environment where love was unpredictable, your nervous system calibrated to that unpredictability as normal. Consistent love feels foreign, even boring. The hot and cold feels like passion, because that was the love template you were handed before you were old enough to question it.

I stayed in relationships longer than I should have, not because I lacked self-respect, but because the version of love I had learned required that kind of pain to feel real. The hot and cold felt like depth. The chaos felt like passion.

That is not a character flaw. That is just a wound doing what wounds do.

Here is the pattern the wound builds. You stop attaching to the reality of who someone is and start attaching to the potential of who you imagine they could become. The relationship is built not on what is, but on what you see underneath, what you believe is waiting to come out if you just love them well enough, pray long enough, stay patient enough, sacrifice yourself completely enough.

The person does not have to be a monster for this to be a trauma bond. They only have to be inconsistent. They only have to be unable to give what they keep almost giving, someone whose words promise what their behaviour cannot deliver, whose moments of tenderness make you doubt the longer pattern of absence or cruelty. The bond is built in the gap between what they are and what you believe they could be, and that gap is where you have been pouring every drop of yourself for years, trying to close it from your side because their side has not been doing the closing.

So let me give you the principle that sets women free from this faster than any other I know. Love the reality, not the potential. The reality of a person is what they are actually doing today, with the resources they actually have, given the choices they have actually been making for the last six months or six years. The reality is not what they say they want to do, what they promise they will do, what they could do if conditions were different, or what they will do once you have helped them through one last hard season. We were trained to see the diamond in the rough and to weigh a person's finest moments against the person standing in front of us, and we were told that this is love. It is not love. It is projection. And no relationship can be healthy when one party is in love with a fiction.

Notice who is tired. The other person is comfortable in their reality. They have lived there all along. You are exhausted, because you have been carrying the weight of their potential while they rest in their reality, and that disparity is the very engine of your depletion. You cannot love a person into their potential. Potential is between them and God. Reality is what you are meant to evaluate.

"Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come: The old has gone, the new is here."

2 Corinthians 5:17 NIV
Part 3

The container with holes

Imagine you have spent twenty years pouring water into a container, faithfully, refilling it every time it empties, working harder and harder because something in you cannot bear to see it dry. The water keeps running out and you keep pouring, and the running-out has become so normal that you stopped questioning whether the container is the problem.

One day a wise woman walks past and watches you pour, and she says gently, "The container has holes in the bottom."

You look. You have always seen the holes. But you have been telling yourself that if you just pour faster, pour more, pour with more love, eventually the level will rise. You have been blaming yourself for the emptying. You have been calling your exhaustion a failure of devotion.

The problem was never the amount of pouring. The problem is the container. Some people are not capable, at this moment in their lives, of holding the love you have been pouring. The holes are not your fault, and they were there before you ever started pouring. No amount of additional pouring closes them, because holes like these are closed only by God, working in the heart of a person who has surrendered them to Him.

There is one more piece of this to name, because it explains why you can understand all of it intellectually and still not leave. Potential is a drug. When you have tasted who someone can be at their best, your brain stores that version as the real one and files everything else as the exception. The flashes of the better version function exactly like any other addictive substance. They give you a hit of what could be, they keep you reaching and they make the long stretches of the actual relationship tolerable, because the brain is convinced that the real person is just around the corner, that the next breakthrough will be the one that sticks. The hope of them becomes the drug you use to numb the disappointment of them.

Think about this. The flashes are not a forecast. The real person is the version that has been showing up most days of your life with them, and the flashes are the exception. The woman who can finally see that, who can grieve the person she was waiting for and accept the person who has actually been in front of her, has taken her freedom back.

Part 4

The loyalty that belongs to God

The patience you have been pouring into someone who keeps failing you was never meant for them in the first place. The hope you have been sustaining through years of almost-changes and half-promises was meant for the Lord, not for a person who has been collecting it and converting it into permission to stay exactly who they are. The faithfulness you have been showing a relationship that shows no faithfulness back is faithfulness God designed you to show Him.

The whole book of Hosea is actually built on it. God told the prophet to marry a woman who kept returning to her bondage, and the marriage became a picture of what His own people were doing to Him, giving their devotion to gods who could not love them while the God who could was waiting in covenant for their return. The trauma bond is, at its spiritual root, a misplaced covenant. You have been making a covenant in your heart with a person who has not entered it with you, while the God who has entered covenant with you watches His portion of your loyalty being handed to someone who cannot steward it.

"Do not be yoked together with unbelievers. For what do righteousness and wickedness have in common? Or what fellowship can light have with darkness?"

2 Corinthians 6:14 NIV

The principle reaches beyond marriage. There are yokes a woman of God is not meant to wear, and wearing them makes her less of who God made her to be, not more, no matter how loyal the wearing has felt. The command to come out is not cruelty. It is the kindness of a Father who has watched His daughter being slowly diminished by a yoke that was never meant for her neck, telling her she has His permission to step out from under it.

"Therefore I am now going to allure her; I will lead her into the wilderness and speak tenderly to her. There she will respond as in the days of her youth, as in the day she came up out of Egypt."

Hosea 2:14-15 NIV

Look at God's pattern with the woman who has been in bondage. It is not condemnation. It is courtship. God does not shame her for the years she spent there. He leads her into a wilderness, away from what was binding her, and there He speaks tenderly to her about who she truly is. The wilderness is not punishment. It is the quiet space where the grip of the old attachment finally loosens, because the bond is no longer being reinforced, and the woman remembers what it felt like to be free. And the wilderness can begin in your spirit before it begins in your circumstances. The withdrawal of your emotional pouring, the redirection of your prayers, the honest naming of the reality you have been excusing. He will meet you there.

Part 5

How to break the bond

You do not break a trauma bond through willpower. You break it by building something strong enough to replace it. A new identity rooted in God, a nervous system slowly retrained by consistent spiritual discipline, and the practice, repeated until it becomes a pathway, of trusting the still small voice instead of the chaos.

"After the earthquake came a fire, but the Lord was not in the fire. And after the fire came a gentle whisper."

1 Kings 19:12 NIV

God speaks in a whisper. Not in the chaos, not in the consuming fire, not in the earthquake. If your nervous system has only been trained to hear love in the dramatic, in the intense, in the consuming, you will keep missing the still small voice that is actually leading you to freedom.

So the practice looks like this. Every time you feel the pull to defend them in your own mind, to make excuses for them to others, to take responsibility for what is theirs to carry, pause and turn that loyalty toward God instead. "Father, this loyalty belongs to You. I am giving back what I have been misplacing." And every time the hope rises that this time will be different, every time the flash of the better version flickers through your mind, bring it to God and leave it there. "Lord, here is the version of them I have been carrying. I cannot make them become this. I never could. I am leaving this hope with You."

Two things happen when you do this. The weight comes off your shoulders, because the work of their becoming was never yours to carry. And the prayer becomes something God can actually work with, because His Spirit can move on a heart in ways your striving never could. You may even see the very transformation you were trying to force, but only after you stop forcing it, because the work was always His and the striving was only in His way.

Freedom from the trauma bond is not found in hating the person. It is found in becoming so rooted in your own identity that their approval becomes unnecessary.

"Now the Lord is the Spirit, and where the Spirit of the Lord is, there is freedom."

2 Corinthians 3:17 NIV

Questions to sit with this week

One. Is there a pattern you keep repeating in relationships? The same type of person, the same cycle, the same ending?

Two. Which relationship in your life are you maintaining on the potential of the person rather than the reality of how they have been showing up?

Three. When did you first experience love as something unpredictable? How old were you?

Four. What container in your life has holes, and have you been blaming yourself for the emptying instead of recognizing that the vessel cannot hold what you have been giving it?

Five. What would consistent, calm love feel like? Does it sound appealing, or does something in you distrust it? What would you need to believe about yourself in order to leave for good?

One honest question

This week, just one practice. Ask yourself one honest question and answer it without softening, without explaining, without protecting anyone, including yourself.

If nothing about this person changed from today forward, if the version of them you have been seeing for the last twelve months is exactly the version you will be relating to for the next twelve, can you live the kind of life God called you to live alongside them? Yes or no.

The honesty of that answer will begin a work in you that nothing else has been able to begin. You do not have to act on the answer this week. You only have to let yourself know it. The knowing is what unlocks every step that comes after.

The Father who loves you is not asking you to stay in what is breaking you. He is calling you out, gently and patiently, into a freedom He paid the highest price to give you. The moment you start to honor the reality instead of the projection, the bond begins to lose its grip, and the woman God formed begins to breathe again.

A prayer for this episode

God, I am caught in something I cannot get out of on my own.

I understand now that the bond is not love. It is a wound looking for resolution in the same place that created it.

I confess that I have been pouring into containers with holes and calling my exhaustion devotion. I confess that I have been waiting for a version of someone who has not been appearing, and weighing my whole life on the waiting.

Retrain my nervous system. Teach me to feel safe in stillness. Help me to hear Your whisper instead of chasing the chaos.

I take back the loyalty I have misplaced and I give it to You, where it has always belonged. Give me the courage to choose the spring of living water over the broken cistern, even when the cistern feels more familiar.

Amen.
