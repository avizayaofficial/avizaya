School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 5

How abandonment creates addiction.

The addiction was never the problem. It was your best attempt at survival. Now you have access to something that actually heals.

"I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never go hungry, and whoever believes in me will never be thirsty."

John 6:35 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

I want to talk about the thing that is destroying women quietly. The addiction that does not look like addiction, the eating disorder that is not really about food, the overworking that is not really about ambition.

They are all the same thing. They are all the same attempt to fill the same hole. And the hole was made by abandonment.

I know this because I lived it. For years I had an eating disorder that I did not even recognise as one while I was in it. I used food to numb what I could not name. By the time I was thirteen I had become a master at making myself not feel. It was the only self-defence mechanism I owned. And it almost killed me.

Part 1

What addiction actually is

Addiction is not a moral failure. It is a pain management strategy. It is what a person found that made the unbearable feel bearable, for a moment.

The eating disorder was not about food. It was about control in a life that felt out of control, about punishing a body that felt like the reason people left, about numbing a pain that had no name yet.

You do not love alcohol. You love what alcohol does to the place inside you that hurts. You do not love bingeing. You love the six minutes when you cannot feel anything else. You do not love the scrolling or the overworking or the constant giving. You love the relief, the temporary exit from the ache you have been carrying since you were a child.

"So if the Son sets you free, you will be free indeed."

John 8:36 NIV
Part 2

The biology of abandonment and addiction

When a child experiences abandonment, the brain's reward system is disrupted. The nervous system goes into a state of chronic dysregulation. And it begins searching, desperately, for anything that will create even a temporary feeling of safety or relief. Food, alcohol, relationships, overwork, overgiving, scrolling. Any port in a storm.

The science of the wound Abandonment activates the same neural pathways as physical pain. Addiction is the brain's attempt to self-medicate that pain. This is not weakness. It is biology. And biology, touched by the power of God and the renewal of the mind, can change.

"Do you not know that your bodies are temples of the Holy Spirit? You were bought at a price. Therefore honour God with your bodies."

1 Corinthians 6:19-20 NIV

Your body is not your enemy. It is a temple. And what has been happening inside it is not evidence of your failure. It is evidence of a pain that was never tended to.

The first step toward freedom is not willpower, it is understanding. You need to know what you are actually hungry for.

Part 3

The counterfeit bread

"I am the bread of life. Whoever comes to me will never go hungry, and whoever believes in me will never be thirsty."

John 6:35 NIV

Every addiction is a counterfeit bread. It fills you for a moment, and then the hunger comes back worse than before. It has to, because you were not hungry for food or alcohol or attention. You were hungry for something that only one source can provide.

I remember the moment I finally understood this. I had been bingeing again. The cycle was so familiar by then that I had stopped even feeling ashamed of it. I was just numb. And in that numbness I had a thought that changed everything. What am I actually hungry for? Not the food. What is the real hunger?

The answer that came was simple. I was hungry to be safe, to be seen, to be loved without having to earn it. Those were needs that no amount of food was going to meet. But they were real needs, sacred needs. And the moment I could name them, I could finally begin to take them somewhere that could actually satisfy them.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What is your counterfeit bread? What do you reach for when the pain becomes too loud?

Two. When did the addiction start? What was happening in your life at that time?

Three. What are you actually hungry for? Not the substance or the behaviour. The underlying need.

Four. Have you ever brought that real hunger to God and asked Him to satisfy it?

Five. What would your life look like on the other side of this? What are you fighting for?

A prayer for this episode

Jesus, I am hungry. Not for what I have been reaching for. For something that nothing I have tried can give me.

I have been feeding the wound with things that cannot heal it. I am tired of being hungry.

You said You are the bread of life. That whoever comes to You will never go hungry. I am coming to You.

Meet the real need. The one underneath the behaviour. Satisfy what nothing else has been able to satisfy. And give me the courage to put down the counterfeit.

Amen.
