School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 3

Healing from father abandonment.

Your father may not have stayed. But you have a Father who was already there before he left. And He has never stopped calling you His own.

"A father to the fatherless, a defender of widows, is God in his holy dwelling. God sets the lonely in families."

Psalm 68:5-6 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

The father wound is the wound that shapes everything else. How you see God, how you see men, how much you will accept before you walk away.

I want to go there today. Not gently. Fully. Because you cannot heal what you are still protecting.

If your father left, was absent, was violent, was emotionally unavailable, or simply was not the father you needed him to be, a part of you made a decision about what that meant. About you, about men, about whether you deserved to be chosen and protected and stayed for. And that decision has been running your life ever since, mostly without your knowledge.

Part 1

What a father was supposed to give you

A father is a daughter's first encounter with the masculine. He is the first man who tells her she is beautiful, valuable, worth protecting. He is the first safe place she learns to run to. He is the first model she uses to understand what a man does when he loves a woman.

When that is missing, she does not just lose a parent. She loses the template. She goes into every relationship she ever has without the internal model of what safe male love looks like. And then she spends years trying to find it in places it was never meant to live.

"As a father has compassion on his children, so the Lord has compassion on those who fear him."

Psalm 103:13 NIV

God uses the father image over and over in Scripture. Not because it is easy. Because He knows what was meant to be there and was not. He is not trying to replace a person. He is trying to restore a function. The function of being unconditionally seen, protected and cherished by a safe masculine presence.

Part 2

How the father wound shows up in your relationships

You chase unavailable men because unavailability is familiar. You work endlessly for male approval because you learned early that approval had to be earned. You leave before you can be left because your body learned that men eventually go. You stay too long in wrong relationships because you are still hoping that this one will be the one who finally stays.

None of this is weakness. It is a nervous system doing exactly what it was trained to do. The question is not whether you learned this pattern. The question is whether you are willing to unlearn it.

The science behind the wound Research on attachment consistently shows that a father's presence or absence shapes a daughter's relationship patterns into adulthood. This is not a character flaw. It is neurobiology, and neurobiology can be rewired. Romans 12:2 is not just theology. It is neuroscience dressed in Scripture.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV
Part 3

The father who never left

"A father to the fatherless, a defender of widows, is God in his holy dwelling. God sets the lonely in families."

Psalm 68:5-6 NIV

He sets the lonely in families. Not just spiritually. Practically. He will bring people into your life who father you in the ways you needed. A mentor, a pastor, a community. People who see you and call out what is in you and stay.

But first, before any of that can work, you have to let God be what your earthly father was not. And that means going to Him with the wound instead of performing your way around it. It means telling Him the truth. God, I needed a father and I did not have one. And it has cost me so much.

He can handle that truth. He has been waiting for you to bring it to Him.

Part 4

Forgiveness is not excusing what they did

"Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing."

Luke 23:34 NIV

Jesus said this from the cross, in more pain than you or I will ever understand. Before anyone apologised, before anyone changed, He released them in the middle of the wound.

Forgiveness is not a feeling. It is a decision. It is not saying what happened was acceptable. It is refusing to let what happened continue to govern your future.

Your father's failure is not yours to carry for the rest of your life. You get to put it down. Not for him. For you. For every future relationship that deserves a version of you that is not still trying to prove something to a man who was not there.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What did your father's absence or presence teach you about your worth? Write it without editing.

Two. Where do you see the father wound showing up in your relationships right now?

Three. Have you ever brought this wound to God specifically? Not as theology. As a personal grief?

Four. Is there something you are still waiting for your earthly father to give you that only God can give?

Five. What would your life look like if you truly released him? Not excused him. Released him.

A prayer for this episode

Father God, I am bringing You a wound I have carried for a long time and mostly in silence.

I did not have the father I needed. And I have spent years trying to fill that space with things that could not hold it.

I ask You today to be what was missing. To fill what was left empty. To restore the function in me that was never given its foundation.

And I choose today to release my father from the debt I have been holding. Not because it was right. Because I want to be free. I want my future back from a man who already took my past.

Restore the years. Restore the template. Show me what safe love actually feels like.

Amen.

Your father may not have stayed.
But you have a Father who was already there before he left.
And He has never stopped calling you His own.
