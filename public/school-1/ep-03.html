School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 3

Healing from father abandonment.

Your father may not have stayed. But you have a Father who was already there before he left, and He has never stopped calling you His own.

"A father to the fatherless, a defender of widows, is God in his holy dwelling. God sets the lonely in families."

Psalm 68:5-6 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

The father wound is the wound that shapes everything else. How you see God, how you see men, how much you will accept before you walk away.

I want to go there today, not gently but fully, because you cannot heal what you are still protecting.

If your father left, was absent, was violent, was emotionally unavailable or simply was not the father you needed him to be, a part of you made a decision about what that meant. About you, about men, about whether you deserved to be chosen and protected and stayed for. And that decision has been running your life ever since, mostly without your knowledge.

We are not walking into this teaching the way the world walks into it. The world walks into the father wound looking for someone to blame, looking for a story to tell about why a woman's life turned out the way it did. We are walking into it looking for what God is doing inside the very place that broke us, because the Word of God is unflinchingly honest about wounds, and it is equally unflinching about what God does with them.

I will tell you where I enter this teaching, so you know I am not speaking from theory. I grew up without a father, but my father did not leave me. He died when I was very small, and I have no memory of him at all. The wound of someone choosing to go came into my story through my mother, when she chose to move away, and we will walk into that wound together later in this school. For a long time I told myself the absence of my father was not really a wound, because I had never known what I was missing, and how can a woman grieve someone she never had the chance to meet. But the absence shaped me anyway. The shape of what was not there was its own kind of presence, and it formed me as surely as a present father would have, only in the opposite direction. So whether your father left by his own choice, was taken by death or was in the house and still never really there, this teaching is for you. The principle holds for every shape of the absence.

Part 1

What a father was supposed to give you

A father is a daughter's first encounter with the masculine. He is the first man who tells her she is beautiful, valuable, worth protecting. He is the first safe place she learns to run to. He is the first model she uses to understand what a man does when he loves a woman.

When that is missing, she does not just lose a parent. She loses the template. She goes into every relationship she ever has without the internal model of what safe male love looks like, and then she spends years trying to find it in places it was never meant to live.

"As a father has compassion on his children, so the Lord has compassion on those who fear him."

Psalm 103:13 NIV

God uses the father image over and over in Scripture. Not because it is easy for the wounded daughter to read. Because He knows what was meant to be there and was not. He is not trying to replace a person. He is trying to restore a function, the function of being unconditionally seen, protected and cherished by a safe masculine presence.

Part 2

The wound beneath the wound

There is a layer of this that psychology can describe, and it is true as far as it goes. The father wound shapes your sense of safety, your sense of worth, your sense of being chosen. But there is a deeper layer underneath, and we have to name it if we want to heal at the root and not just at the surface.

The father wound is, at its heart, a wound to your image of God.

Every child first learns what father means from her own father. He is the first translator of the heavenly Father's nature. By his daily presence or absence, by his words or his silences, by the way he looks at her or the way he overlooks her, he teaches her what fathers are like. And when she grows up and is introduced to the Father in heaven, she reads Him through the translation she was given on earth.

If the earthly father was absent, the daughter grows up quietly assuming God is also absent. If the earthly father was harsh, she quietly assumes God is also harsh. If the earthly father was disappointed in her, she quietly assumes God is disappointed in her too. If the earthly father chose other things over her, she quietly assumes God will also choose other things over her.

This is the deepest cut. Not that your earthly father failed you. He was a man, and men sometimes fail. The deepest cut is that his failure has been silently translating itself into your relationship with the Father in heaven, and you have been operating in your faith for years through a corrupted translation without knowing it. Until that translation is corrected, your spiritual life will keep hitting an invisible ceiling, because you cannot fully receive from a Father you secretly suspect of being like the man who hurt you.

And yet the wound is also the doorway. The women I have watched walk most deeply into the Father heart of God are almost never the women who had wonderful earthly fathers. They are the women who carried this exact wound, because the wound created in them a hunger that women with full earthly fathers rarely have, and that hunger drives them into the only One who can satisfy it. The exact wound the enemy meant to use against you is the one that has been making your heart hungry enough to actually receive what God is offering. What the world calls your disqualification, the kingdom calls your qualification.

Part 3

How the wound shows up in your life

You chase unavailable men because unavailability is familiar. You work endlessly for male approval because you learned early that approval had to be earned. You leave before you can be left, because your body learned that men eventually go. You stay too long in wrong relationships, because you are still hoping this one will be the one who finally stays.

None of this is weakness. It is a nervous system doing exactly what it was trained to do. The question is not whether you learned this pattern. The question is whether you are willing to unlearn it. Research on attachment consistently shows that a father's presence or absence shapes a daughter's relationship patterns into adulthood. That is not a character flaw. It is neurobiology, and neurobiology can be rewired.

"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind."

Romans 12:2 NIV

The wound also comes in many shapes, and I want to name a few of them, not to dwell there but so you can find yourself in the naming, because being seen in the wound is the beginning of being healed of it. Some fathers leave by divorce, by addiction, by their own choice or by their own inability to stay, and some are taken by death, and the daughter carries the absence into every relationship after. Some fathers are present in the home, but their presence is a threat instead of a refuge, and the daughter learns that to be loved she has to perform and to be safe she has to please. Some are there in body but absent in attention, paying the bills without ever paying attention, and the daughter learns to settle for proximity without intimacy, which later shows up as men who are near her but not actually with her. Some fathers have their hearts stolen by alcohol, work, other women or status, and the daughter learns she cannot compete with whatever has a man's heart. And some chose another family, and the daughter learned she was not enough to keep a father, and she has been quietly preparing to be left ever since.

If you are reading this thinking, this is not me, I had a good father, thank God for him, because what he gave you is rarer than you know. But ask yourself one gentle question before you set this teaching aside. Did anyone else who held a father role fail at it in a way that left you wondering what was wrong with you? A stepfather, a grandfather, a pastor, a coach, a teacher, even God Himself as He was misrepresented to you by someone who painted Him in a way that did not match His heart. And there is the daughter who was loved but not protected, the one who became her father's confidante and emotional partner because no one else would be, who lost her father-daughter relationship to a role reversal her family forced on her. That is also a father wound, and it sits in a woman quietly until someone names it and gives her permission to grieve what she did not know she had lost.

Part 4

The Father who runs

"A father to the fatherless, a defender of widows, is God in his holy dwelling. God sets the lonely in families."

Psalm 68:5-6 NIV

God has named Himself two things in that verse. A father to the fatherless and a defender of widows. He has chosen, by His own voice, to position Himself with the full weight of who He is exactly where a man failed to be. He does not consider this charity. He considers it His holy assignment. And He sets the lonely in families, not just spiritually but practically. He will bring people into your life who father you in the ways you needed, a mentor, a pastor, a community, people who see you and call out what is in you and stay.

But before any of that can work, you have to let God be what your earthly father was not. And that means going to Him with the wound instead of performing your way around it. It means telling Him the truth. God, I needed a father and I did not have one, and it has cost me so much. He can handle that truth. He has been waiting for you to bring it to Him.

"I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you."

John 14:18 NIV

This is Christ Himself, speaking on the night before His death to followers who were about to feel like they were losing everything. He did not say eventually you will find another father figure, and He did not say I am sorry this is happening. He said I will come. The Father has been on His way to you since before you knew you were missing Him.

Look at how Jesus described Him in the parable of the prodigal son. The son had taken his inheritance early, the deepest insult a son could deliver in that culture, and wasted everything, and in his ruin he decided to come home hoping to be taken on as a hired servant, because he had concluded he no longer deserved to be a son.

"But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him."

Luke 15:20 NIV

The father saw him while he was still a long way off, which means the father had been watching the road. He had not given up, had not moved on, had not built a new life that excluded the child who left. And when he saw him, he ran. A dignified man in that culture did not run. Running was for children and laborers. But this father picked up his robes and ran, because receiving his child home mattered more to him than his own dignity. The son had a speech prepared and never got to finish it. The father interrupted him with the robe, the ring and the celebration, because no child of his comes home to be made a servant.

That is the heart of God toward you. He is not standing with His arms folded, judging the distance you have left to travel. He has been running toward you since before you knew you needed to come home. The version of the Father you have been afraid of, because of what your earthly father was, is not the Father who is actually coming down the road.

"I will be a Father to you, and you will be my sons and daughters, says the Lord Almighty."

2 Corinthians 6:18 NIV

I will be. A future tense from God is more certain than a past tense from any man. And the Spirit He has given you teaches your heart the most intimate word in the language, Abba, the word a small daughter cries when she has scraped her knee. Not Lord, though He is Lord. Not King, though He is King. Abba. I know how that lands in a woman with a father wound. It feels too intimate, too vulnerable, too tender for the Creator of the universe. But when you can say Abba to God and mean it, when you can climb into the lap of the Father in heaven without performing or apologizing or making yourself useful first, the father wound has begun to heal at the root, and not just at the surface where the world tries to heal things.

Part 5

FOR-giveness, the gift that was given to you

Now we come to the part of this healing that no one can do for you, and I want you to see it in a way you may never have seen it before.

Look at the word itself. Forgive. For-give. To give away. That is what the word has been trying to tell us all along. Forgiveness is not a feeling you wait for. It is a giving away.

Think about what is actually connecting you to the person who hurt you. It is not love anymore, and it is not presence. The only thing keeping you connected to them now is the pain. That pain is the cord, and behind that pain is a spirit, because pain like this does not appear from nowhere. For them to cause you that pain, the pain had to live inside them first. They did not invent it. They received it somewhere, they carried it, and then they multiplied it, manifested it and shared it with you. That is how this thing travels, from generation to generation, from wound to wound. And if you never deal with it, you will do exactly what they did. You will multiply it into the people around you, into your children, into your relationships, or you will spend your life sending it back at the one who gave it to you, and either way it keeps living, and it keeps feeding.

So you rebuke that pain. You refuse to give it a home. And the way you disconnect from the person is by disconnecting from the pain, and the way you disconnect from the pain is forgiveness. You give the pain away. You hand it to God, who is the only one strong enough to carry it and the only one with the right to judge the one who caused it.

This is what changes everything. Forgiveness is not a gift you hand the person who hurt you. It is a gift God gave to you, so that you can release and detach yourself. The world has taught us that forgiving means saying it was acceptable, and so we hold on to the unforgiveness like a form of justice. But unforgiveness is not justice. It is the cord that keeps you tied to the very person you are trying to get free of. Forgive means give it away. It was never meant to stay in you.

"For if you forgive other people when they sin against you, your heavenly Father will also forgive you. But if you do not forgive others their sins, your Father will not forgive your sins."

Matthew 6:14-15 NIV

God did not attach forgiveness to your freedom to burden you. He attached it because He knows how the pain operates. He knows that the woman who keeps the pain becomes the woman the pain keeps.

Jesus showed us what this looks like at the point of maximum injury.

"Father, forgive them, for they do not know what they are doing."

Luke 23:34 NIV

He said this from the cross, in more pain than you or I will ever understand. Before anyone apologized, before anyone changed, He released them in the middle of the wound. Forgiveness is not a feeling. It is a decision. It is not saying what happened was acceptable. It is refusing to let what happened continue to govern your future.

Your father's failure is not yours to carry for the rest of your life. You get to put it down. Not for him. For you, and for every future relationship that deserves a version of you that is not still trying to prove something to a man who was not there. And if your father is gone from this earth and there is no conversation left to have, hear this. Forgiveness never needed his participation. It happens between you and God, which means it is still fully available to you, today.

"And when you stand praying, if you hold anything against anyone, forgive them, so that your Father in heaven may forgive you your sins."

Mark 11:25 NIV

When you stand praying. That is where the giving away happens. Not in a confrontation, not in a perfect apology you may never receive. In the presence of the Father who never left, you open your hands and you give the pain away.

Questions to sit with this week

Read one a day. Sit with it, and bring it to God in prayer.

One. What did your father's absence or presence teach you about your worth? Write it without editing.

Two. How has that absence quietly translated itself into your image of God? Where has the wound been showing up in your spiritual life without you knowing it was the wound?

Three. Where do you see the father wound showing up in your relationships right now, in who you chase, who you tolerate and who you leave before they can leave you?

Four. Is there something you are still waiting for your earthly father to give you that only God can give?

Five. What is the pain that still connects you to him, and what would your life look like if you gave it away this week? Not excused it. Gave it away.

One word, spoken once

This week, just one practice. Find a quiet place. Speak the word Abba out loud, to God, one time. Just once is enough to begin. Then sit with whatever rises in you afterward. Maybe tears, maybe resistance, maybe nothing at all the first time, and that is also alright, because the work is happening even when you cannot feel it happening.

The word will not stay strange forever. Every time you speak it you are practicing a new instinct, and the instinct that needs to be formed in every daughter of God is the instinct of crying Abba in moments of need and in moments of joy and in the small quiet moments of an ordinary Tuesday afternoon when no one is watching the practice forming inside you.

He is the Father you have been looking for your whole life. The only thing that has been standing between you and receiving Him fully is the translation you were given by another man, and that translation is being corrected in you right now, as you read this, whether you can feel it or not.

A prayer for this episode

Father God, I am bringing You a wound I have carried for a long time and mostly in silence.

I did not have the father I needed, and I have spent years trying to fill that space with things that could not hold it. I confess that I have let that wound translate itself into who I assumed You to be, and I have been reading You through a translation You never wrote.

I ask You today to be what was missing. To fill what was left empty. To restore the function in me that was never given its foundation.

And I choose today to for-give. To give away the pain that has been the last cord connecting me to what happened. I rebuke that pain, I refuse to multiply it into the people I love and I place it in Your hands, because You are the only one strong enough to carry it. I release my father from the debt I have been holding. Not because it was right. Because I want to be free, and because You gave me forgiveness as a gift for my own release.

Teach me to call You Abba and to mean it. Restore the years. Restore the template. Show me what safe love actually feels like.

Amen.
