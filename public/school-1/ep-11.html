School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 11

The hardest forgiveness: forgiving yourself.

The only one still holding your past is you. God put it as far as the east is from the west. You can let it go.

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Romans 8:1 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

We talk about forgiving others and we rarely talk about forgiving yourself. And in my experience, the self-forgiveness is harder. Far harder. Because the person you need to forgive is the one you cannot get away from.

You cannot leave the room and come back when you are ready to speak to yourself. You cannot take space from yourself. You are with you all the time. And if the relationship with yourself is one of judgment, condemnation and an endless record of failures and wrong choices, that background noise runs underneath everything you do.

You have built a court inside your own mind, and you have been the prosecutor, the jury and the judge all at once. The verdict has been guilty, the sentence has been long, and the punishment has been the constant low-grade self-rejection you have carried into every relationship and every season of your life. You do not even hear the verdict being spoken anymore, because it has been running under your thoughts for so long that you have confused it for your own voice. It is not your voice. It is an old judgment you have been refusing to overturn, and today the overturning begins.

Part 1

What self-condemnation actually is

Self-condemnation is not humility. I know it feels like humility. It feels like being appropriately honest about your failures, like not letting yourself off the hook too easily.

But what it actually is, is pride. It is the insistence that your failures are too great for grace. That you are the exception to the redemption that is available to everyone else. That God can forgive others but not the specific things you have done.

That is not humility. That is arrogance dressed as self-awareness. It is telling God that what Jesus did on the cross was sufficient for everyone except you.

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Romans 8:1 NIV

No condemnation. Not reduced condemnation, not conditional condemnation. None. The verse does not say there is no condemnation if you have it together. It says there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. And if you are in Christ, you are already covered.

So whose voice has been making the case against you? Scripture names him by his title.

"For the accuser of our brothers and sisters, who accuses them before our God day and night, has been hurled down."

Revelation 12:10 NIV

The accuser is not a metaphor. There is a voice that says look at what you allowed, look at who you became, look at how much time you wasted. It gives you no credit for the growth and no acknowledgment of the strength it took to reach this moment. It only catalogs the failures. And there is the voice of the Father, which says something else entirely. Look at how far you have come, look at what you have survived, look at what I have been forming in you, even in the seasons you thought I had forgotten you.

Both voices speak when you catch your own reflection. The work is not to silence the accusation through force of will. The work is to agree with the Father's voice so consistently that the accusing voice loses its authority, because the accuser only has power as long as you keep agreeing with him.

Part 2

The judgments you have been carrying

Before you can forgive yourself, you have to name what you have been holding against yourself, because the judgments you carry are often unconscious, and the unconscious ones do the most damage.

You have been holding it against yourself that you stayed too long. In the relationship, in the friendship, in the job, in the season you now know was costing you your peace. You walk past the version of yourself who stayed and you shake your head at her, and the head-shaking has become its own quiet self-violence, repeated every time you remember what you allowed.

You have been holding it against yourself that you gave too much. You rehearse the time, the money, the energy, the love you poured into people who poured none of it back, and the rehearsing makes you feel like a fool. But your giving was never the failure. The placement was.

You have been holding it against yourself that you did not see the signs. You replay the moments when, in hindsight, the truth was obvious, and you punish the version of yourself who did not yet have the eyes to see it. You forget that vision is built over time, and that what is obvious to you now was not obvious then, because you had not yet lived through what made it obvious.

And you have been holding it against yourself for the numbing, the avoidance, the years spent quieting what you did not yet know how to feel. You have been calling that survival weak, when it was the very thing that kept you alive long enough to reach the moment you could heal.

Now hear the reframe that ends the long inner argument. You were not stupid. You were loyal, hopeful and loving, and you did not deserve what happened to you. A heart that could keep showing up, keep giving and keep believing that love could be redemptive was never the problem. The problem was where you placed it, and the placement is what you are learning to do differently. As long as you believe you were a fool, you will keep punishing the woman who made those choices. The moment you see that she was loyal and hopeful and simply did not yet have the discernment you have now, the punishment loses its rationale, and forgiveness becomes possible for the first time.

Part 3

The things I had to forgive myself for

I had to forgive myself for staying when I knew I should have left. For giving money and time and love and emotional capacity to people who were not going to be careful with it. For making choices from my wound that I would never have made from wholeness.

I had to forgive myself for the eating disorder. For the years I spent hating my body and punishing it for being the reason people did not stay.

I had to forgive myself for the 14-year-old who stayed behind when her mother moved away and then watched her family fracture around the decision. I had to look at that girl and understand that she was scared and stubborn and trying to hold on to the only world she knew. And I had to stop punishing her for being human.

There was a long season when I could not look at myself in the mirror. I would walk past my reflection and avert my eyes, because what I saw in the glass was a woman I had been judging for years. The Lord met me in that mirror, in a season when I had finally exhausted my own ability to punish myself, and He showed me I had been holding a verdict against myself that He had never agreed with. Forgiving myself was harder than forgiving anyone who ever hurt me. But the freedom on the other side of that work is unlike anything else I have walked into. The woman who has been forgiven by herself walks differently. She receives differently, she loves differently, and she no longer performs her way into worthiness, because she has accepted the worthiness the Father placed in her from the beginning.

Did I make the mistake or did the mistake make me? Both. And both are forgivable.

One more piece of my own confession, because the deepest layer of self-forgiveness involves admitting something harder than the failure itself. Somewhere quietly inside, I liked being the one who gave everything and got nothing. The martyr is a clean identity. It makes you the innocent one, it hands you the moral high ground, and it lets you feel injured and righteous at the same time. When I was the giver, I was in control, and I never had to be vulnerable enough to just receive. True vulnerability is not giving. True vulnerability is receiving without a receipt, letting someone else pay, being seen in your need. The deepest self-forgiveness came when I forgave myself for the sweetness I had found in the pain. Not because the pain was good. Because admitting that part of me was choosing the dynamic was the only way to step out of it for the rest of my life. This is not self-blame. It is self-compassion in its deepest form, because it restores your agency, and the restoration of agency is the doorway the martyr identity cannot enter. Let the martyr die. She got you out alive, but she cannot enter the promised land of reciprocity, and reciprocity is where God is calling you next.

Part 4

For-give yourself

Earlier in this school, when we walked through forgiveness, I gave you the word the way the Lord gave it to me. Forgive means for-give. To give away. Forgiveness is not a feeling you wait to arrive. It is the deliberate giving away of what you have been holding, so that it no longer has a place to live inside you.

Now turn that word toward yourself. The pain you have been holding against yourself is the last thread tying you to the woman you were. Self-forgiveness is giving that pain away to God rather than carrying it as penance. It is not excusing yourself. It is releasing yourself. And it is a gift God already gave you, not one you must earn. Christ paid the full price for every failure you have ever committed, which means the forgiveness you have been refusing to extend to yourself is forgiveness the Father has already extended to you. Your work is not to manufacture it. Your work is to receive it and pass it on to the woman in the mirror.

"Then Peter came to Jesus and asked, 'Lord, how many times shall I forgive my brother or sister who sins against me? Up to seven times?' Jesus answered, 'I tell you, not seven times, but seventy-seven times.'"

Matthew 18:21-22 NIV

Seventy-seven is not really a number. It is a way of saying without limit, and the woman in the mirror does not need to be forgiven once. She needs to be forgiven again every time the old accusation returns, every time the memory surfaces, every time the regret tries to climb back into the present. Forgive her again, and forgive her again, until forgiveness becomes the dominant voice and the accusation becomes the voice that lost its place.

There is a sobering piece at the end of that same chapter. When the servant who had been forgiven an unpayable debt refused to pass the mercy on, his master handed him over to the jailers until he could pay back everything he owed. And the parable leaves a question hanging. How does a man in jail pay back a debt? He cannot, because the imprisonment itself makes the repayment impossible. The same is true of the woman in the spiritual jail of self-condemnation. As long as that spirit lives inside her, she cannot earn her way out, because the way out was never earning. The way out is passing the same grace forward, including to yourself. You give away the grace that was given to you, and the giving away is what opens the jail.

"Be kind and compassionate to one another, forgiving each other, just as in Christ God forgave you."

Ephesians 4:32 NIV

Never call this forgiveness cheap. It flows freely to you now only because the price was already paid in full, and extending it to yourself will cost you something too. It costs you the case you have spent years building against yourself. The case feels precious because you built it so carefully, but it is the very thing keeping you imprisoned, and laying it down is the price of your freedom.

Two clarifications are important to make. Forgiving yourself does not mean approving of what you did. Some acts stand as wrong. Forgiveness is not extended to the act, it is extended to the person, because God has already extended it to the person. Your job is to forgive the woman, not to relitigate the acts. And do not confuse forgiveness with trust. Forgiveness is given freely. Trust is rebuilt over time, by keeping the small promises you make to yourself, by showing up in the daily choices that prove you are now the woman you say you are. You can forgive yourself fully today and still spend a season rebuilding trust with yourself. Both are required, and they must not be confused.

Part 5

The Father's verdict

"As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us."

Psalm 103:12 NIV

The east and the west do not meet. There is no point on the map where one becomes the other. The distance is infinite by design, and that is the picture David gives for how far the Father has placed your failures from your person. They are not in a folder He is keeping for later. They are not in a file the accuser can subpoena. They have been removed, and the removal is permanent. The only one still holding them is you.

And yet so many daughters of God spend years revisiting what the Father Himself has put away. Digging up what He has already buried. Prosecuting what He has already pardoned. Living in a constant inner courtroom while the Father stands outside the door saying, "Daughter, come out. There is no trial. I closed the case before you ever opened it."

"If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just and will forgive us our sins and purify us from all unrighteousness."

1 John 1:9 NIV

The promise is certain. And Paul says that if anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come, the old has gone and the new is here, 2 Corinthians 5:17. The woman you used to be is not the woman you are now. Self-forgiveness is simply the work of catching your inner narrative up with what God has already declared about you. The old woman is the one the accuser keeps dragging to the front of your life, but she has no current standing, and you do not owe her another day of your attention.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What have you never fully forgiven yourself for? Name it without the performance of minimising it.

Two. How has self-condemnation been running your life? Where does it show up in your choices?

Three. Is there a version of yourself from the past that you are still punishing? How old is she? What did she need that she never received?

Four. Where have you been calling yourself stupid for being loyal, hopeful or loving? What is God saying about those very qualities?

Five. Has the martyr identity been giving you something you have not yet been willing to release? What would reciprocity ask of you instead?

The two letters

There is a practice the Lord gave me in a heavy season of self-forgiveness, and it is the most concrete tool I have found for moving this work out of theory and into your actual life. You write two letters.

The first letter is from your current self to the woman who stayed. The woman who allowed what she allowed, who did not yet see what you now see. You do not write to scold her. You write to see her. Something like this.

I see you, and I see why you stayed. You were not stupid. You were loyal, hopeful and loving, and you were doing the best you could with what you knew at the time, and what you knew at the time is not what you know now. You were pouring into a container that could not hold what you were giving, and the failure was the container, not the pour. I forgive you. I release you, and I lay down every judgment I have been carrying against you. You did not deserve what happened, and you do not deserve to keep being punished for it by the woman you have become. You are safe with me now, and you are free.

The second letter is from your future healed self, looking back at the woman you are right now. She has lived through everything you are about to live through, and she has come back to tell you how it turns out.

I am writing to you from the other side of this season, and I need you to hear this. You make it. You become someone you did not know you could become. You learn to discern what is real love and what is not, to give from fullness instead of emptiness, to receive what the Father has been holding out to you for years. The pain you are in right now becomes the very wisdom that helps other women out of theirs, and nothing you are walking through is wasted. I love you, and I am proud of you. Keep walking, because the other side is closer than you think.

Set aside one quiet hour this week, alone with God, and write both letters in your own words. Read them out loud when you finish, and keep them somewhere you can return to the next time the accuser tries to come back. Then stand in front of a mirror, once, and look yourself in the eye the way you would look into the eyes of a daughter you love, and say slowly: I forgive you, I see why you stayed, you did not deserve what happened and I lay down every judgment. The woman in the mirror has been waiting for this longer than you know, and the only person whose forgiveness can reach that deep is you.

A prayer for this episode

God, I bring You the things I have never fully let go of. The choices I made from a wounded place. The things I let happen to me. The ways I hurt myself and others while I was trying to survive.

I lay down the verdict I have been holding against the woman I used to be, the verdict You never agreed with. I give the pain away to You today, because it was the last thread tying me to her, and I am not her anymore.

I receive the forgiveness You have already given. Not just intellectually. In my body. In the place where the shame lives. As far as the east is from the west. Help me to believe that distance. Help me to stop dragging it back close.

Amen.
