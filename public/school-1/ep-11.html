School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 11

The hardest forgiveness: forgiving yourself.

The only one still holding your past is you. God put it as far as the east is from the west. You can let it go.

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Romans 8:1 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

We talk about forgiving others and we rarely talk about forgiving yourself. And in my experience, the self-forgiveness is harder. Far harder. Because the person you need to forgive is the one you cannot get away from.

You cannot leave the room and come back when you are ready to speak to yourself. You cannot take space from yourself. You are with you all the time. And if the relationship with yourself is one of judgment, condemnation and an endless record of failures and wrong choices, that background noise runs underneath everything you do.

Part 1

What self-condemnation actually is

Self-condemnation is not humility. I know it feels like humility. It feels like being appropriately honest about your failures, like not letting yourself off the hook too easily.

But what it actually is, is pride. It is the insistence that your failures are too great for grace. That you are the exception to the redemption that is available to everyone else. That God can forgive others but not the specific things you have done.

That is not humility. That is arrogance dressed as self-awareness. It is telling God that what Jesus did on the cross was sufficient for everyone except you.

"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."

Romans 8:1 NIV

No condemnation. Not reduced condemnation, not conditional condemnation. None. The verse does not say there is no condemnation if you have it together. It says there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus. And if you are in Christ, you are already covered.

Part 2

The things I had to forgive myself for

I had to forgive myself for staying when I knew I should have left. For giving money and time and love and emotional capacity to people who were not going to be careful with it. For making choices from my wound that I would have never made from wholeness.

For the eating disorder. For the years I spent hating my body and punishing it for being the reason people did not stay.

I had to forgive myself for the 13-year-old who stayed behind when her mother moved away and then watched her family fracture around the decision. I had to look at that girl and understand that she was scared and stubborn and trying to hold on to the only world she knew. And I had to stop punishing her for being human.

"Did I make the mistake or did the mistake make me? Both. And both are forgivable."

Part 3

What self-forgiveness actually requires

Self-forgiveness requires that you stop using your past as evidence against your present. It requires that you separate who you were from who you are. It requires that you agree with what God already says about you: that your failures are not the final word about you, and that every single mistake you have ever made has already been covered by something larger than your ability to mess up.

"As far as the east is from the west, so far has he removed our transgressions from us."

Psalm 103:12 NIV

As far as the east is from the west. That is not a small distance, that is an infinite distance. That is God saying: I have put it so far from you that it is no longer in the vicinity of how I see you. The only one still holding it is you.

Questions to sit with this week

One. What have you never fully forgiven yourself for? Name it without the performance of minimising it.

Two. How has self-condemnation been running your life? Where does it show up in your choices?

Three. Can you hear Romans 8:1 as a personal statement to you rather than a general theological claim?

Four. Is there a version of yourself from the past that you are still punishing? How old is she? What did she need that she never received?

Five. What would change in your present if you actually let go of that past?

A prayer for this episode

God, I bring You the things I have never fully let go of. The choices I made from a wounded place. The things I let happen to me. The ways I hurt myself and others while I was trying to survive.

I receive the forgiveness You have already given. Not just intellectually. In my body. In the place where the shame lives.

As far as the east is from the west. Help me to believe that distance. Help me to stop dragging it back close.

Amen.
