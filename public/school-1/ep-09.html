School 1: The Abandoned Girl  ·  Episode 9

When love becomes a weapon

You are not the abandoner. You are the woman who finally stopped abandoning herself. Those are not the same thing.

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It does not dishonor others, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs."

1 Corinthians 13:4-5 NIV  ·  Anchor verse
Before we begin

I need to tell you about a woman who found me on the internet at two in the morning.

She had been in a relationship with a man who promised he would never fail her. And then he did, over and over. And she kept forgiving him, and staying, and losing pieces of herself in the staying, until one night she typed into a search bar the words she could not say out loud to anyone she knew.

I know that woman. Not because I read her message. Because I was her.

So I want to talk today about what happens when love is used as a weapon, when the person who was supposed to protect you uses your love for them to take from you instead. This dynamic is common enough that almost every one of us has lived inside some version of it, and disguised enough that almost none of us had the language to name what was happening while it was happening. Love is one of the most powerful forces there is. God Himself is named love by the Word, not as a description of His feelings but as a description of His very being. And anything that powerful can, in the hands of a wounded person, be used not to build but to break. The same words and gestures that were designed to make you more of yourself can be used to make you less of yourself, and the cruelest part is that the vocabulary of love is the very thing doing the wounding, so you cannot recognise the wounding for what it is.

This matters for everything else in this school. You can do all the inner work in the world, but if you return each night to a dynamic where affection is being used as a tool of control, the work will not stick, because it cannot heal what continues to wound you in the very place it is trying to heal.

Part 1

When love is weaponised

Weaponised love looks like this. They know you are loyal, so they exploit your loyalty. They know you forgive, so they keep doing the thing that requires forgiving. They know you will stay, so they stop working to deserve your staying. They know you love them deeply, so they use that depth as leverage.

Stay or I will suffer. Stay or I will fall apart. Stay or you are the one who is abandoning everyone.

And because you have an abandonment wound, the accusation lands perfectly. You cannot bear to be the one who leaves. You have lived your whole life trying not to be the one who abandons. So you stay, and you keep pouring, and they keep taking what you pour and calling it love.

Understand what makes this so hard to see. The wounding part is not the absence of affection. The wounding part is that the affection itself has become the tool. A person who simply ignored you would be easier to leave than a person who says I love you in the same breath they use to tear you down, because the affection is the very thing keeping you loyal to the wound. Weaponised love confuses the nervous system in a way no other harm can, because the body cannot file what is happening into a single category. Half of the body registers love and says stay. The other half registers harm and says flee. The conflict between the two signals creates a kind of paralysis, and you have probably been interpreting that paralysis as your own failure to leave, when really it is your body responding accurately to a situation designed, intentionally or not, to keep you trapped between two truths.

And the person using love as a weapon is not always doing it consciously. Many wounded people learned this language in their own families and are repeating, without awareness, what was done to them when they were small. That does not make the harm less real, but it explains why confrontation rarely changes the pattern. The pattern is older than this relationship. It is the water they have been swimming in their whole life, and they cannot see the water any more than a fish can see the ocean.

Your work is not to fix the wounded person doing the wounding. That is between them and God. Your work is to recognise what is happening, name it clearly and refuse to let what has been called love keep defining your understanding of what love actually is. It is not love if it requires you to disappear. It is not love if it demands you betray yourself to maintain it. What it is, is a wound that found your wound and learned how to use it.

Part 2

The cycle and its shapes

Weaponised love almost always runs in a predictable cycle, and once you can see the cycle clearly, you can never again be confused by the moments of warmth that follow the moments of harm.

The first stage is idealisation. He adores you. He mirrors your faith. He says all the right words, tells you he has never met a woman like you, and you believe him, because the attention is so intense and so specific that it could not possibly be a script. The relationship feels destined.

The second stage is devaluation. He starts to find fault. He mocks the things he once admired and twists your goodness into accusations. The woman he praised last month is the woman he criticises this month, and you do not understand what changed, because you have not changed.

The third stage is the discard or the threat of it. He belittles you, tells you that you are a waste of his time, threatens to end the relationship or ends it temporarily. You are lucky I put up with you. I do not know how much more of this I can take. Maybe I should just go. Each one is a different way of using the fear of losing him to control you.

The fourth stage is the return. He softens. He apologises, says he was angry, says he is worried about you, says you mean everything to him. The version of him from the beginning briefly reappears, relief floods your body and you are pulled back in.

Then the cycle turns again, and each turn costs more. The tender phases get shorter with every iteration and the harsh phases get longer, and every round takes more peace and more recovery than the one before. The cycle is not love and harm in equal measure. It is harm being slowly normalised through occasional moments of relief, and the relief is what your nervous system has been mistaking for love. Real love does not cycle. Real love does not require recovery periods. Real love has no idealisation phase, because real love is consistent, and consistency does not need to manufacture intensity through periodic withdrawal.

Alongside the cycle, weaponised love comes in shapes worth recognising on sight. Criticism disguised as care, where the words sound like guidance and the cumulative effect is that nothing about you is ever quite right. The silent treatment used as punishment, where absence is deliberately deployed to teach you what behaviour will cost you their love. Conditional love, where affection appears when you please them and disappears when you do not, so the relationship becomes an audition with a bar that moves. Public warmth beside private cruelty, so convincing in front of others that you start to wonder if you are making the private version up, and so isolating that no one in your life would believe you. Surveillance disguised as concern, where every checking question sounds protective on the surface and possessive underneath. Healthy love says I hope you are okay. Weaponised love says, in a hundred different sentences, I need to know what you are doing so I do not lose control of you. And spiritual manipulation, where God, Scripture and the language of faith are used to shame you, to question your boundaries, to make you doubt yourself. Real spiritual connection draws you toward peace and discernment, never toward confusion and guilt.

One more thing, and it matters. These patterns do not appear only in romantic relationships. A mother can weaponise love against an adult daughter. A friend can weaponise love against a friend. A mentor can turn spiritual care into spiritual control. Wherever affection is being used as a tool of diminishment, everything in this episode applies.

Part 3

The mirror and the fruit

Real love does not make you smaller. It does not ask you to abandon your values to stay in it. It does not leave you more depleted after every interaction. It does not require you to hide your pain to protect theirs. It does not make you feel crazy for having needs.

"Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It does not dishonor others, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no record of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres."

1 Corinthians 13:4-7 NIV

Hold this passage up to the relationship in question. Not as a weapon. As a mirror. Paul was not writing a sentimental verse for wedding ceremonies. He was writing the most rigorous definition of love the Word offers, and every line is a measuring stick. Does this relationship honour you? Does it seek your good as much as it seeks its own? Does it keep score? Does it record your wrongs and use them to keep you compliant? If what has been called love in your life has not looked like this passage, then the Word is naming it plainly. It was not love. It was something else wearing the costume of love.

"There is no fear in love. But perfect love drives out fear, because fear has to do with punishment. The one who fears is not made perfect in love."

1 John 4:18 NIV

Think about this. If you are afraid of someone, you are not in love with them. You are in survival. Real love produces peace and safety, not vigilance and chronic fear. If you are constantly anxious in the presence of someone who claims to love you, if you are walking on eggshells around them, what you are calling love is something else. And your body has been telling you the truth all along. The vigilance you feel before they arrive is not your problem to manage. The tightness in your chest when you hear their voice is not immaturity. The relief you feel when they are not in the room is not a character flaw. These are diagnostic signals your nervous system has been sending for months or years, and the work now is to finally listen to them instead of overriding them in the name of trying harder.

"Better is open rebuke than hidden love. Wounds from a friend can be trusted, but an enemy multiplies kisses."

Proverbs 27:5-6 NIV

This is one of the sharpest diagnostics in all of Scripture, and it is counterintuitive enough that many of us missed it for years. The warmth of a surface is not the measure of love. A friend who tells you a hard truth out of genuine care is loving you. Someone who showers you with affection while quietly working against you is harming you, and the affection itself is the camouflage. The constant declarations, the over-the-top gestures, the multiplied kisses can be the very evidence of an enemy at work.

So do not measure the surface. Measure the fruit. Jesus said it Himself: "By their fruit you will recognize them." The fruit of real love is a more whole woman, a more confident woman, a more peaceful woman. The fruit of weaponised love, no matter how affectionate the surface, is a smaller woman, an anxious woman, a woman who used to be more of herself than she is now. If the fruit of the relationship is the diminishment of who you were when it began, the relationship is not loving you, regardless of how warm its surface has been.

Ask yourself one honest question. When do you feel God's presence stronger, when you are near this person or when you are away from them? Which version of you do they awaken, the calm and radiant one, or the anxious and apologetic one? Your peace is evidence of truth. God does not send His daughter into relationships that suffocate her spirit, and if your spirit goes quiet around someone, that is information you are meant to act on.

Part 4

Choosing yourself is not abandonment

Here is the lie the wound tells you. If you leave, you are just like everyone who left you. You are becoming the abandoner and you are repeating the pattern.

This is the most sophisticated trap the wound sets, because it uses your healing against you. It uses the very values you are trying to live by to keep you in a place that violates those values.

Choosing yourself is not abandonment. Walking away from something that is destroying you is not the same as what was done to you when you were a child. You are not a 13-year-old girl who had no choices. You are an adult woman who does. And using that choice is not betrayal. It is the highest form of self-respect available to you.

There is a companion lie that has to fall with it, and it is the one the church has sometimes blessed without meaning to. Altruism is not holiness. Being so selfless that you allow others to use you is not an act of care, because you cannot possibly care for others if you do not care for yourself. When you are so kind to everyone that you allow anyone to walk over you, you are simply disrespecting yourself, and there is nothing noble about a generosity that costs you your dignity. Altruism can even be the mirror image of the very selfishness it thinks it opposes. One person hides a hidden sense of unworthiness behind grandiosity and takes because he believes he deserves to. Another hides the same unworthiness behind endless giving, and gives until she is empty because she does not yet believe she deserves to receive. Both are running on the same root, and both produce relationships that drain instead of build.

And when a daughter of God allows herself to be mistreated in the name of being patient or long-suffering, she is not helping anyone. She is feeding the very pattern that is wounding her, and the pattern will go on to wound others through her silence. The kindest thing you can do for a wounded person is refuse to let them wound you any further. Long-suffering toward someone who is consistently harming you is not the long-suffering God commends in His Word, and the love He has for you is not asking you to keep being broken so that someone else can keep being comfortable.

"I have come that they may have life, and have it to the full."

John 10:10 NIV

A full life. Not a depleted life, not a life spent pouring into someone who takes everything and returns nothing. Jesus came so that you could have abundance, and abundance is not compatible with a relationship that drains you to the bones and calls it love.

Part 5

What real love looks like

The clearest way to recognise the counterfeit is to know the real thing so well that the counterfeit becomes obvious by contrast.

Real love is reciprocal. The flow goes both ways, the giving feels mutual and the result is two people being built up by what passes between them, not one person slowly drained while the other is fed. Real love is safety. You feel calmer in their presence, not more vigilant. Your nervous system can rest around them, and the steadiness becomes the foundation the whole relationship is built on. Real love studies you. It is curious about who you are, pays attention to what you say and remembers what matters to you, because it is engaged with the soul of you, not just the function you serve. Real love brings the best out of you. The version of you that emerges in the relationship is more confident, more free and more alive than the version that walked in. The relationship is the soil you are growing in, not the box you are being kept small inside. And real love is patient with your becoming. It does not demand you arrive at some finished state before it deems you worthy. It walks with you through your unfinished places and trusts God to keep doing in you what only He can do.

Real love is also built on earned intimacy, not instant vulnerability. Your softness is a privilege, not a test. Give it to those who show consistency over time, and watch how a person handles small conflicts before you offer them the deepest parts of you. The one who responds with gentleness in the small things will respond with gentleness in the large ones. The one who twists your words in the small things will twist them in the large ones.

And real love shares your faith in fruit, not just in label. Scripture warns against being unequally yoked, and the principle goes far beyond religious affiliation. Two people cannot pull the same plough in opposite directions and expect the field to be worked. When one heart is operating from humility, forgiveness and grace, and the other from ego and control, the relationship stops being about partnership and becomes about power. If you sensed early on that the alignment was not there and overrode the sense because you wanted to believe the best, take the lesson forward, not as never trust again, but as trust your spirit's first discomfort. The Holy Spirit does not confuse or condemn. He simply withdraws His peace when something is not right, and that withdrawal is one of the clearest signals you have.

I have lived inside the dynamic this episode names, and I learned the long way the difference between a love that builds and a love that takes. What broke the pattern for me was not a confrontation. It was finally trusting what my body had been telling me for years, bringing what I was sensing to God and letting Him show me the truth my own heart had been overriding in the name of being patient. And this is the line that set me free, so I am giving it to you. Love did not abandon you. Someone who could not match it did. Love is not the problem. Counterfeit love is the problem, and counterfeit love is not love at all.

Questions to sit with this week

One. Is there a relationship in your life right now where you are staying because of fear rather than genuine love?

Two. When you measure that relationship against 1 Corinthians 13, where does it match and where does it fail? What is the honest evaluation telling you that you have been refusing to hear?

Three. Which of the shapes of weaponised love do you recognise most in your own life, and where are you confusing loyalty with self-abandonment?

Four. When do you feel God's presence stronger, near this person or away from them? What is that signal telling you that you have been afraid to act on?

Five. What are you most afraid will happen if you stop making yourself small to keep this relationship?

Three movements for this week

The first movement is the 1 Corinthians 13 diagnostic. Take a quiet hour and write the lines of the passage down the left side of a page. Patient. Kind. Not envious. Not proud. Not dishonouring. Not self-seeking. Not easily angered. Keeps no record of wrongs. Protects. Trusts. Hopes. Perseveres. Beside each line, write honestly whether the relationship you are evaluating fits that line in actual daily practice over the last twelve months. Not in the best moments. In daily practice. Do not soften the evaluation. The Word did not soften the definition, and you cannot afford to soften the application. Then sit with whatever the page shows you, in the presence of God, and ask Him what He is asking you to do with it.

The second movement is to listen to the body. For one week, pay attention to the signals your body sends about the people in your life. When you are about to be in their presence, notice what your shoulders do. When you hear their voice, notice what happens in your chest. When you anticipate seeing them, notice what your breath does. The body has been keeping the score of every interaction, and none of what it reports is a moral failure on your part. It is your nervous system telling you what your conscious mind has been overriding, and the work of this week is to finally listen.

The third movement is to stop financing what is not feeding you. You do not have to make dramatic declarations or deliver any speeches. You only have to begin, quietly, to withdraw the investment that has been propping up the dynamic. Stop overexplaining yourself. Stop chasing approval. Stop performing for their moods. Stop pouring emotional, spiritual and even financial resources into what has not been giving back. The pouring is the fuel the cycle runs on, and the slow withdrawal of that fuel is the slow unwinding of the bond.

A prayer for this episode

God, give me the clarity to see my relationships the way You see them.

Show me the difference between love and a wound wearing love's clothing. I confess that I have been overriding the signals You placed in my body to keep me safe. I confess that I have called altruism holiness and depletion devotion.

Teach me to read the fruit instead of the surface, and to trust the peace of Your Spirit over the intensity of any feeling I have learned to call love.

Give me the courage to choose what is right over what is familiar, what is life-giving over what is comfortable. And protect me from the lie that choosing myself is the same as abandoning others.

I release what You are asking me to release, and I receive the love You are preparing in its place.

Amen.

Those are not the same thing.
